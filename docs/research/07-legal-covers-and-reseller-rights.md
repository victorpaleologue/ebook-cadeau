## 1. Executive summary

### 1.1 Findings

1. **A reseller owns nothing in a cover.** Its right to show a cover is contractual. The working assumption is a licence limited to "promotion and sale" of the title, but **no reseller contract was seen** (unverified). Such a licence is read under ordinary contract interpretation (C. civ. arts. 1188 ff. [M]), not under CPI L. 131-3: that formalism protects authors in the contracts they sign themselves and does not govern contracts between an assignee and its sub-exploiters (Cass. 1re civ., 13 Oct 1993, "Perrier" [M]). The limit comes from upstream: the publisher can pass on only what the illustrator or photographer granted it (L. 131-3, L. 122-7 [M]). France has no general advertising exception. Loi n° 2011-590 regulates price only and is silent on covers [V1].
2. **Printing a cover on a card is a reproduction that needs its own basis** (CPI L. 122-3; Directive 2001/29 art. 2 [M]). Art & Allposters (C-419/13 [V2]), an exhaustion case, illustrates that a change of medium needs fresh consent; it is not needed to reach the conclusion. Whether a store-display licence stretches to print is arguable and untested: no French decision was found.
3. **The retailer's usual comfort does not transfer to eBooks.** Dior v Evora (C-337/95 [V2]) lets a reseller advertise goods it lawfully resells, but it rests on exhaustion, and eBooks have none (Tom Kabinet, C-263/18 [V2]). Advertising copies is itself an act within the distribution right (Dimensione Direct Sales v Knoll, C-516/13 [M]). For eBooks the contract is the only basis (inference, untested).
4. **Standalone use has no exception.** The EU exception list is closed [V2]; French short quotation does not cover a full image, even reduced [V2]; re-posting a freely available image is a new act (Renckhoff, C-161/17 [V2]). US fair use has been accepted for search thumbnails [V2]; that it would not stretch to a full-size printed jacket is analysis, not a holding.
5. **The only statutory foothold is private copying by the end user**, from a lawful source, on equipment the user holds. In French doctrine the copier is the party that holds the equipment and exploits it by making it available to customers: the copy shop, not the customer who presses the button (Rannou-Graphie, Cass. 1re civ., 7 March 1984 [M on the wording; one weak secondary source]). A provider that runs the copying cannot borrow its users' exception (Wizzgo [V1, one source]; VCAST, C-265/16 [V2]; Ocilion, C-426/21 [M]). With composition in the browser the user is **arguably** the copier. This lowers the risk; it is not a proven shield, and it holds only when the user prints on a printer of their own. A PDF taken to a copy shop, a kiosk or an office printer makes that operator the copier in France. (Unverified at source.)
6. **Moral rights cannot be cleared by the publisher alone.** Cropping, mirroring, colourisation and cut-outs of photographs are recognised infringements [V2]. Overprinting a cover and extending artwork onto a spine are alterations of the same kind by extrapolation (analysis; no decision found). The author may consent to specific, identified modifications, but cannot waive the right in general [M]. A cover must be reproduced untouched; the 10 mm spine, the back and the QR panel must be separate panels built from our own material.
7. **Text is safe.** Title, author and publisher name retyped as plain text, in our own layout and openly licensed fonts, is low risk in every mode and every jurisdiction examined (assessment). The layout must be our own: imitating a collection's look is exposed to parasitisme and, for recent designs, to the EU unregistered design right (finding 11).
8. **Every precedent found is rights-holder opt-in.** Enthrill, Walmart–Kobo, Juniper Books and Out of Print all work on publisher agreements, own artwork or public-domain material [V2/V1]; Out of Print has belonged to Penguin Random House since 2017 [M]. No case of a retailer unilaterally printing cover art was found, and no litigation against these businesses. Both "none found" statements describe 31 searches, not the world: French and German precedents were not searched (unverified).
9. **Two regulatory side-issues are open** [M]. *Prime*: a free card that is printed and mailed is plausibly a "prime" under art. 4 of loi 2011-590; a free print-at-home PDF probably is not; a paid optional card is not a prime, since a prime is free. The delivery-vehicle and packaging arguments are untested. The *médiateur du livre* is the body that could settle the point. *VAT*, conservative position: an optional, separately priced card is a distinct supply at 20 %, and the eBook stays at 5.5 %; separate pricing is a strong indicator, not conclusive. A code for one eBook is a single-purpose voucher only if redemption is limited to one Member State; an EU-wide code is arguably multi-purpose. Details in `09-legal-gifting-and-commerce.md`.
10. **Largest unknowns**: the actual reseller-facing terms of Eden Livres, Immatériel/7switch, Numilog, Interforum, Hachette and Kobo/Fnac; the cover-licence terms of Dilicom/FEL and Electre; whether a gift to a third party is "usage privé du copiste". Facts about the named retailers were not rechecked: whether 7switch still trades, and whether the Fnac–Kobo arrangement is unchanged in 2026.
11. **Three layers were missing from the draft** [M]. (a) *Design law*: the EU unregistered design right protects the appearance of a product, graphic works and get-up included, against copying for three years; it reaches recent covers, collection looks and typographic covers that fail the originality test (§2, §6). (b) *Collective management*: where the illustrator or photographer is a member of ADAGP or SAIF, the right needed may sit with the society, and a publisher opt-in may be ineffective (§2). (c) *Technical protection*: private copying never permits circumventing a protection measure, so the tool must never decrypt an eBook to reach its cover (§4.2).
12. **"Public domain" is a per-country test** [M]. Death of the artist plus 70 years holds for France, Germany and most of the EU. Spain keeps 80 years for authors who died before 7 December 1987. In the United States the term of older works runs from publication: a cover published in 1931 or later can still be protected in 2026 whatever the artist's death date. A co-authored cover is counted from the last surviving co-author (§8.1).
13. **Naming a retailer is not the trade-mark problem; imitating it is** [M]. A descriptive mention of "Fnac" or "7switch" is referential use. The exposure lies in copying a logo, a site's look or a look-alike name (§6).

### 1.3 Decisions recommended

- **Default output in every mode is the typographic card.** Artwork is an upgrade that needs a recorded rights basis.
- **Printed covers in reseller mode require a per-title flag** backed by a written publisher authorisation, plus a warranty and indemnity from the reseller.
- **Standalone covers are user-supplied, client-side, print-at-home only.** The service never fetches a cover into a printable file, never decrypts an eBook to reach one, and never sends an unlicensed cover to a printer. "At home" means the user's own printer; the interface says so.
- **Covers are never modified.** The spine is typeset, not derived from the artwork.
- **Public-domain artwork is gated per country**, not by one "death + 70" rule.
- **A free printed card is not offered in reseller mode** until the prime question is cleared; a free print-at-home PDF may be.
- **Demo stores carry invented brands.** Real retailers may be named descriptively in documentation; their logos, look and look-alike names are not used.

Three points follow.

1. **Permission runs through the publisher, but is not exhausted by it.** Covers are protected works; authorisation is to be sought from the publisher, who knows the rights holders [V2] ([Guichet du Savoir](https://www.guichetdusavoir.org/question/voir/45700), [Pierrat](https://www.village-justice.com/articles/tirer-couverture,54716.html)). Litigation over photographs used by publishers does occur; one headline seen but not read: [ActuaLitté, À bout de souffle / Flammarion](https://actualitte.com/article/132591/droit-justice/photo-du-tournage-d-a-bout-de-souffle-la-justice-blanchit-flammarion).
2. **Lack of originality is not a safe harbour in France.** CA Versailles, 22 March 2022 (Zulma v Elior) denied copyright in the cover of "L'Embellie" for lack of demonstrated creative choices, but still found **parasitisme** for its reuse in an advertising campaign — [Fournol 1](https://www.fournol-avocat.fr/actualite/2022/8/17/la-difficile-reconnaissance-de-loriginalit-des-couvertures-de-livre), [Fournol 2](https://www.fournol-avocat.fr/actualite/2022/8/18/parasitisme-reconnu-pour-la-couverture-dun-livre) [V1]. Both links are the same law-firm blog, so this is one source: the date and the outcome were not read at source and the damages figure was never seen (unverified). If it holds, a "plain" typographic cover is not free to take (C. civ. art. 1240 [M]). The EU unregistered design right points the same way for covers disclosed in the last three years (table above) [M].
3. **The text's status says nothing about the cover.** A public-domain novel in a recent edition carries protected artwork for the artist's life plus 70 years in France (CPI L. 123-1 [M]), counted from the death of the last surviving co-author where the cover has several authors (L. 123-2 [M]); French moral rights survive that. The term is not the same everywhere (§8.1). Photographs are fully protected if they are the author's own intellectual creation (Painer, C-145/10 — [IPKat](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2011/12/all-photos-are-created-equal-painer.html), [Kluwer](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/another-piece-of-the-puzzle-or-is-it-cjeu-on-photographs-as-copyright-works/)) [V2].

Consequences:

- **Is the card a "prime" at all?** The draft assumed so. The sibling commerce note separates three cases, and this document now follows it [sib: M]:

  | Offer | Reading | Risk |
  |---|---|---|
  | Free print-at-home PDF | A presentation of the delivered code, of negligible value, comparable to free gift wrap | Low: probably not a prime |
  | Free card, printed and mailed (cost about EUR 2–7) | A free good with a market value, tied to the sale: a prime, unless the publisher offers it to all retailers on the same terms; or an indirect discount | Medium to high |
  | Paid optional card, own price line, at or above cost | A separate service expressly requested by the buyer; a prime is free by definition | Low |

  Two arguments could take even the free printed card out of the rule, and neither was tested: that the card is the delivery vehicle or usual packaging of the product, and that it is an item of low value. The former Code de la consommation carved such items out of the general prime ban (arts. R. 121-8 to R. 121-10) [M]; whether an equivalent survives after the 2011 liberalisation and the 2016 recodification is unknown. Until counsel or the médiateur du livre clears it, the reseller charges for a printed card as a separate, separately invoiced service, or has the publisher offer it to all resellers. A card priced below cost could also be attacked as a disguised discount on the fixed price. This risk is independent of copyright.
- **VAT** [sib: M]. Conservative position: an optional, separately priced card is a distinct supply at the standard rate (20 % in France), and the eBook keeps its reduced rate (5.5 %). Optional purchase and separate pricing are strong indicators, not conclusive ones: a taxpayer could argue that a paid card is ancillary to the eBook. A free print-at-home PDF is ancillary and adds nothing to the taxable amount. A compulsory "gift pack" at one price risks the highest rate on everything (CGI art. 278-0). A code for one identified eBook is a voucher (Directive (EU) 2016/1065). It is a single-purpose voucher only if the place of supply is known at issue, which means redemption limited to one Member State (CJEU, C-68/23, M-GbR); a code redeemable EU-wide is arguably multi-purpose, with VAT due at redemption. See `09-legal-gifting-and-commerce.md` and `notes/legal-gifting-commerce.commerce.md`.
- **Under a mandat** the retailer sells in the publisher's name and for its account; "le mandataire ne peut rien faire au-delà de ce qui est porté dans son mandat" (C. civ. art. 1989) [M]. An agent has less room for own-initiative promotional formats than a buyer-reseller.

The right to promote flows author → publisher → (e-distributor) → retailer, each step "for promotion", with the publisher as gatekeeper. A retailer clause is assumed to read roughly "to reproduce and display cover art, metadata and excerpts solely to market, promote and sell the eBooks through the store". **This wording is recalled as typical; it is not quoted from a real clause, and no reseller contract was seen (unverified).** Three points decide how far such a clause goes:

- **The reseller's licence is read under ordinary contract law, not under CPI L. 131-3** (correction; the draft said the opposite). L. 131-3 requires each right assigned to be mentioned separately and the field of exploitation to be delimited as to extent, destination, place and duration. That formalism protects the author and governs the contracts the author signs. The Cour de cassation has held that it does not govern contracts between an assignee and its sub-exploiters (Cass. 1re civ., 13 Oct 1993, "Perrier"; pourvoi number recalled as n° 91-11.241, to be checked) [M]. A publisher → distributor → reseller licence is therefore interpreted by the parties' common intention and, failing that, as a reasonable person would read it (C. civ. arts. 1188 ff.); in doubt, a negotiated contract is read against the creditor and a standard-form contract against the party that proposed it (art. 1190) [M]. A licence to display "on the site" still does not obviously include print, but the reason is the wording of the clause, not a statutory rule of strict reading.
- **The limit comes from upstream.** The publisher can pass on only what it holds. Its own contract with the illustrator or photographer is an author's contract, to which L. 131-3 and L. 122-7 apply: what was not expressly assigned stays with the author [M]. Magnard-Vuibert (§2) shows a publisher unable to prove more than purchase orders. A reseller clause, however wide, cannot cure a narrow upstream grant.
- **No general advertising exception.** InfoSoc Directive art. 5(3)(j) lets Member States allow use "for advertising the public exhibition or sale of artistic works". France did not use that option. Its only catalogue exception, for judicial-sale catalogues (CPI L. 122-5 3° d), pre-dates the directive (introduced in 1997, narrowed in 2000 [M]) and is not a transposition of it — [Le Monde du Droit](https://www.lemondedudroit.fr/pi-tic/278-propriete-intellectuelle-industrielle/58593-protection-par-le-droit-dauteur-des-catalogues-dune-maison-de-vente-aux-encheres-mis-en-ligne.html) [V1]. Short quotation does not cover a full image (§4.2).

**Fnac chain** [M]: Fnac's eBook catalogue is operated with Kobo; the publisher licences may sit with Rakuten Kobo, not Fnac. A sub-licence chain publisher → distributor → Kobo → Fnac → eBook Cadeau → printer must be permitted at every link. Neither this arrangement nor the current status of 7switch (Immatériel's store) was rechecked for 2026 (unverified).

### 3.4 Customary tolerance

In practice "les ayants-droit s'opposent rarement à une reproduction de la couverture à un format inférieur à celui de l'ouvrage et dans un but d'information du lecteur" — [Guichet du Savoir 139502](https://www.guichetdusavoir.org/question/voir/139502) [V1]. This is tolerance, revocable at will, not a right. Its two conditions (smaller format, informational purpose) are exactly what a full-size shelf object strains. Commercial use requires asking [V2 on the principle, with [Pierrat](https://www.village-justice.com/articles/tirer-couverture,54716.html)].

### 3.5 Promotion of the sale, or separate merchandising?

Factors a French court would weigh (analysis; no decision found):

| Points toward "accessory of the sale" | Points toward "separate merchandising" |
|---|---|
| Exactly one card per eBook licence sold; never sold alone | Charged separately; produced by a third party for profit |
| The card is the delivery vehicle (it carries the code), like the box of a download-code product | Designed to stay on a shelf after redemption: decorative function |
| The cover identifies the product purchased | Cover at or near full size, print resolution, with a spine: it imitates the book object |
| Licence says promotion "sur tous supports" or "par tous moyens" | Licence says "on the Site/Store"; print not mentioned (ordinary contract interpretation; the upstream grant is limited by L. 131-3 and L. 122-7) |

**Assessment** (the authors' own; unverified). With a licence limited to the online store, printing is outside scope more likely than not. With "tous supports" promotional wording it becomes defensible, more so at reduced size. With an express clause it is clean as against the publisher, though not necessarily against third-party art owners (§2). Note the tension between the first copyright factor and the prime rule (§3.1): charging separately helps on price law and hurts on "accessory of the sale".

### 3.8 Contract clauses (reseller ↔ publisher or e-distributor)

**Check in the existing agreement**

1. Definition of "éléments de promotion", "métadonnées", "matériel promotionnel": are cover, blurb, extract, logos listed?
2. Purpose and **media**: "sur le Site" versus "sur tous supports / par tous moyens"; any mention of print, PLV, catalogues.
3. **Sub-licensing and subcontracting** ("prestataires", "sous-traitants"): needed for the composition service and the printers.
4. Right to **resize, crop, adapt**; credit obligations.
5. Prior-approval clause for promotional operations; under a mandat, the agent's instructions on promotion.
6. Clauses on **primes, bundles, gifts, discounts** (loi 2011-590 art. 4 [M]).
7. Trade-mark licence scope and brand guidelines.
8. Takedown delay and the publisher's own rights warranty over supplied material.
9. Territory (where printed cards ship) and term (cards outlive a title's withdrawal).
10. Any statement on artwork under collective management (ADAGP, SAIF), or on covers the publisher licensed in rather than commissioned [M].

**Add (amendment or side letter)**

- Express authorisation to reproduce the front cover "sur une carte imprimée ou imprimable remise à l'acheteur d'un exemplaire numérique de l'œuvre, à raison d'une carte par exemplaire vendu", directly or through named categories of subcontractors.
- Integrity rules: no cropping or overprint; spine and inside panels composed outside the cover image; mandatory credit line.
- **Per-title opt-in or opt-out** through a feed flag; default exclusion of tie-in, licensed-character and rights-managed covers.
- Publisher warranty that it holds cover rights for this use, or a duty to flag titles where it does not. The warranty names artwork managed by a collective management organisation: either the publisher has cleared the use with the society, or it flags the title [M].
- Whether the publisher supplies the real spine or the flat full-cover file ("couverture à plat", ONIX List 158 code 29), and whether it may be re-fitted to a 10 mm spine.
- Logo use: yes or no, with guidelines. Blurb use: yes or no.
- Statement that a printed card is a paid, optional service with its own price (or a publisher-offered premium available to all retailers), with the publisher acknowledging the pricing structure. "Ancillary" is avoided in this clause: for VAT the conservative position treats the paid card as a distinct supply (§3.1).
- Takedown within 24–48 h for new generation; sell-off rule for cards already printed.

## 4. Standalone use: exceptions by jurisdiction, and who is the copier

### 4.1 EU frame

- **Closed list.** Art. 5 of Directive 2001/29 is exhaustive; neither Member States nor courts can add exceptions, even on fundamental-rights grounds: Funke Medien (C-469/17, para 64), Pelham (C-476/17, para 65), Spiegel Online (C-516/17, para 49), all 29 July 2019 — [Kluwer on Spiegel Online](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/reflections-on-the-cjeus-judgment-in-spiegel-online-is-there-a-golden-intersection-between-freedom-of-expression-and-eu-copyright-law-part-i/), [Kluwer on Pelham](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/sample-sample-in-my-song-can-they-tell-where-you-are-from-the-pelham-judgment-part-ii/), restated in the AG opinion in C-590/23, Pelham II ([FRA](https://fra.europa.eu/en/node/49752)) [V2]. The paragraph numbers were not checked at source, and only the opinion is cited: whether the Pelham II judgment has been delivered was not checked. There is no "it helps sell the book" defence and no EU fair use.
- **Re-posting is a new act.** Renckhoff (C-161/17, 7 Aug 2018): copying a photo freely and lawfully available on one site and posting it on another is a new communication to a new public; the absence of technical restrictions at the source is irrelevant — [IRIS Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/8373), [Columbia GFoE](https://globalfreedomofexpression.columbia.edu/cases/land-nordrhein-westfalen-v-dirk-renckhoff/), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/copyright/730320/the-risks-of-reposting-online-content-cjeu-rules-on-the-unauthorised-reposting-of-copyright-protected-works-on-the-internet) [V2]. "The cover is all over the web" is no defence for a service that fetches and re-serves it.
- **Linking is treated the opposite way** (added by the fact-check; the thumbnail rule in row 13 and implication 19 depends on it). A link or an inline frame to a work that is freely available with the rights holder's consent reaches no new public: Svensson (C-466/12, 13 Feb 2014); BestWater (C-348/13, order of 21 Oct 2014). The position changes if the link circumvents measures against framing that the rights holder adopted or imposed: VG Bild-Kunst (C-392/19, 9 March 2021). It also changes if the source was posted without consent and the linker knew or, acting for profit, is presumed to know: GS Media (C-160/15, 8 Sept 2016). All [M], consistent with `notes/cover-sources.scraping-law.md` [sib: M]. A hotlinked preview is therefore defensible only from a source that shows the cover with consent, without anti-hotlinking protection, and whose terms do not forbid it. It never extends to a printed copy.
- **Private copying through a service.** VCAST (C-265/16, 29 Nov 2017): a cloud service can in principle sit inside the private-copy exception, but the user must have lawful access to the source, and where the provider itself makes the work available without authorisation the exception fails — [Kluwer](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/up-in-the-cloud-some-reflections-on-the-cjeu-judgment-in-vcast/), [IPKat](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2017/11/the-vcast-decision-how-to-turn-private.html), [LK Shields](https://lkshields.ie/news-insights/publication/eu-court-defines-boundaries-for-cloud-based-services-recorded-tv-programmes), [Quintais/Rendas](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/26578344/EU_copyright_law_and_the_Cloud.pdf) [V2]. **Design rule: a tool that helps the user copy something the user already has may be covered; a service that also supplies the work is not.**
- **The later ruling: Ocilion** (C-426/21, 13 July 2023) [M], added by the fact-check. An operator offered commercial customers an online recorder, cloud-hosted or installed on their premises, in which the copy made for the first user was served to every other user who asked for the same programme. The Court held that the private-copy exception does not cover such a provider-organised scheme. It also held that merely supplying on-premises hardware and software, technical assistance included, is not a communication to the public by the supplier. Both halves matter here: supplying a tool is not in itself a restricted act, but the exception is read strictly as soon as the provider organises the copying or one copy serves several users. The browser tool makes one copy, on the user's device, for that user only.
- **A tool can make its supplier a primary infringer** [M], added by the fact-check. Filmspeler (C-527/15, 26 April 2017) and Ziggo (C-610/15, 14 June 2017): a device or platform designed and marketed to give access to protected works is the supplier's own communication to the public. Those cases concern access to works the supplier points to, which our tool does not do (the user brings the file). They still show that design and marketing can move a "mere tool" into primary liability, so the statement that exposure is limited to indirect theories was too confident (§4.7).
- **Lawful source only.** Private-copy regimes cannot cover unlawful sources — [Next](https://next.ink/news/86942-la-justice-europeenne-impose-decouplage-copie-privee-et-source-illicite.htm) [V1]; ACI Adam, C-435/12 [M].
- **No circumvention.** The exception gives no right to defeat a technical protection measure (Directive 2001/29 art. 6; CPI L. 331-5 ff.) [M]. A cover extracted from a protected EPUB by removing its protection is not a lawful private copy.
- **Exhaustion** frees only further distribution of the tangible copy sold; it never authorises a new reproduction (illustrated by Art & Allposters [V2]) and does not exist for eBooks (Tom Kabinet [V2]).
- Art. 5(3)(j) (advertising the exhibition or sale of artistic works, "excluding any other commercial use"), 5(3)(i) (incidental inclusion), 5(3)(k) (parody) [M]: a faithful cover is not a parody (Deckmyn, C-201/13 [M]); the others are treated per country below.
- Art. 17 of the DSM Directive (content-sharing platforms) does not fit: the service does not give the public access to user uploads [M].
- Art. 5(2)(b) limits private copying to natural persons and non-commercial ends; art. 5(5) adds the three-step test [M]. Copydan (C-463/12) accepted that a private copy may be made with a device belonging to a third party [M], which supports the "tool" position.

### 4.2 France (CPI)

- **Principle.** Any reproduction without consent is unlawful (L. 122-4 [M]); contrefaçon is also a criminal offence (L. 335-2: up to 3 years and EUR 300,000 [M]).
- **Short quotation (L. 122-5 3° a) does not work for images.** "La représentation intégrale d'une œuvre, quelles que soient sa forme et sa durée, ne peut s'analyser comme une courte citation": Cass. 1re civ., 4 July 1995, Antenne 2 ([text](https://affairesjuridiques.aphp.fr/textes/cour-de-cassation-civile-1ere-4-juillet-1995-antenne-2-droits-dauteur-courte-citation-representation-integrale-de-loeuvre/)); a photograph reproduced in reduced format is still a full reproduction: Cass. 1re civ., 7 Nov 2006 ([Squire Patton Boggs](https://larevue.squirepattonboggs.com/droits-d-auteur-reproduction-d-une-photographie_a334.html)); same line in Fabris v Loudmer (Cass. 1re civ., 22 Jan 1991 [M]) and the Utrillo/France 2 case (Cass. 1re civ., 13 Nov 2003 [M]; [IRIS Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/284)); summarised by [Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/citations-dartistes) [V2]. The reseller notes cite the same line as "Ass. plén., 5 Nov 1993, Fabris/Utrillo" [M]: this is the plenary decision in the Fabris v Loudmer litigation (Fabris held Utrillo's rights), so the two notes agree; the verified 1995 and 2006 decisions are kept as the primary authorities.
- **Accessory theory.** Cass. 1re civ., 12 May 2011, n° 08-20.651 ("Être et avoir"): illustrations visible in the background of a documentary were an accessory inclusion outside the monopoly — [Squire Patton Boggs](https://larevue.squirepattonboggs.com/etre-et-avoir-ou-les-auxiliaires-du-droit-d-auteur_a1468.html), [Le Monde du Droit](https://www.lemondedudroit.fr/pi-tic/278-propriete-intellectuelle-industrielle/33575-affaire-qetre-et-avoirq-de-linclusion-fortuite-dune-oeuvre.html), [IRIS Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/5813) [V2]. On the card the cover is the main subject of the front panel, so the theory is unavailable (application).
- **Private copy (L. 122-5 2°).** Text: copies "réalisées à partir d'une source licite et strictement réservées à l'usage privé du copiste et non destinées à une utilisation collective" — [Baumann](https://www.baumann-avocats.com/CPI/article-l122-5-du-code-de-la-propriete-intellectuelle.php), [Doctrine](https://www.doctrine.fr/l/texts/codes/LEGITEXT000006069414/sections/LEGISCTA000006179045) [V2]. Three difficulties:
  1. *Who is the copiste?* See §4.7.
  2. *"Usage privé du copiste"*: a card handed to a friend is not obviously the copier's own use. The independent notes found no case and call it unsettled. The sibling note on gifting recalls that in the Aurélien D. litigation (CA Montpellier 2005; Cass. crim., 30 May 2006; CA Aix-en-Provence, 5 Sept 2007) copies lent to friends counted against private use [M]. The point therefore leans against the user, and the tier is rated Amber, not Green.
  3. The 2° carve-out for "copies des œuvres d'art destinées à être utilisées pour des fins identiques à celles pour lesquelles l'œuvre originale a été créée" [M]: a cover reproduced to serve as a cover on a shelf is uncomfortably close. This worry is the drafter's own speculation; no authority applying the carve-out to a cover was found or is recalled.
  4. *Where the file comes from* (added by the fact-check) [M]. The source must be lawful, and the exception does not allow circumventing a technical protection measure (CPI L. 331-5 ff.; circumvention is itself an offence, L. 335-3-1). A cover read from an unprotected or watermarked EPUB the user bought is unproblematic. A cover obtained by stripping an eBook's protection is not. A cover saved from a retailer's web page raises that retailer's terms of use, which were not read. Whether reading a cover image that a protected EPUB leaves unencrypted counts as circumvention was not analysed; the tool treats any encrypted package conservatively (implication 37).
  5. *Printing outside the home* (added by the fact-check) [M]. Copies made at a copy shop fall under the reprography regime (CPI L. 122-10 ff.): publication of a work entrusts the reprographic right to an approved society (the CFC), and its licences cannot authorise copies made for sale, rental, advertising or promotion without the author's consent. Whether a print from a digital file is "reprographie" was not checked. Either way the copy shop, not the user, is the copier (§4.7).
- **No general equivalent of art. 5(3)(j)**: France did not use that option; the only catalogue exception, for judicial-sale catalogues (L. 122-5 3° d), pre-dates the directive [V1 for the exception, M for its history; §3.2].
- **Licence scope** (corrected): a reseller's right to display a cover on its product page does not by itself include printing it on a gift object. The reason is ordinary contract interpretation and the limits of the publisher's own upstream grant, not CPI L. 131-3, which governs authors' contracts only (§3.2) [M].
- **Litigation climate.** Damages set at three times the evaded licence fee for a photo reused on a third-party site (TJ Bordeaux, 26 June 2025, per [Kohen Avocats](https://kohenavocats.com/concurrent-copie-site-internet-textes-photos-fiches-faire-cesser-chiffrer-2026/)): one secondary source, decision not read, unverified. Systematic reuse of auction-catalogue content led to a contrefaçon and parasitisme judgment against Artprice ([UGGC](https://www.uggc.com/condamnation-dartprice-com-pour-contrefacon-et-concurrence-parasitaire/)) [V1]. Moulinsart obtained an infringement finding over reproduction of Tintin cover elements (Cass. 1re civ., 26 May 2011, n° 09-71.083, [Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/juri/id/JURITEXT000024085051/)): snippet only, the decision was not read, unverified.
- **Leads not checked** (added by the fact-check) [M, low]: La Martinière v Google (TGI Paris, 18 Dec 2009), on unlicensed scanning and display of book content, and SAIF v Google (CA Paris, 26 Jan 2011), on image-search thumbnails. Whether book covers were part of either holding is not known; both should be read before relying on any "display is tolerated" argument in France.

### 4.3 Germany (UrhG, [statute](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/BJNR012730965.html))

- **§ 51 (Zitat)**: a Bildzitat needs an inner link between image and text; a cover used as decoration next to a review does not qualify, and bloggers "in der Regel" hold no rights in the cover — [WBS 1](https://www.wbs.legal/it-und-internet-recht/verwendung-der-fotografie-eines-buchcovers-im-rahmen-einer-buchrezension-ohne-zustimmung-des-urhebers-14143/), [WBS 2](https://www.wbs.legal/internetrecht/fotorecht-bildrecht/verwendung-der-fotografie-eines-buchcovers-im-rahmen-einer-buchrezension-ohne-zustimmung-des-urhebers-32292/) [V1]. Author credit is a substantive condition of the quotation defence (LG Köln, 14 O 291/24, per [Ferner](https://www.ferner-alsdorf.de/lg-koeln-notwendigkeit-der-urheberangabe-bei-der-berufung-auf-die-zitatschranke/)) [V1] (one secondary source; decision not read).
- **§ 58 (Katalogbildfreiheit)**: lets the organiser (gallery, art dealer, auction house) reproduce works publicly exhibited or intended for public sale, as far as needed to promote the event — [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/id/beitrag/2-urheberrecht-c-werke-in-ausstellungen-HI15189406.html), [§ 58 text](https://lexmea.de/de/gesetz/urhg/58) [V1]. It concerns selling the artwork itself. An analogous application to covers is rejected as soon as the site has a commercial purpose ([WBS](https://www.wbs.legal/internetrecht/fotorecht-bildrecht/verwendung-der-fotografie-eines-buchcovers-im-rahmen-einer-buchrezension-ohne-zustimmung-des-urhebers-32292/)) [V1]; traders who advertise with book-cover images "tragen ein gewisses rechtliches Risiko" and rely on lived practice ([IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/Urheberrecht-Versto%C3%9F-Produktfotos-Produktbeschreibung-Werke.html)) [V1]. § 58 gives nothing for a printed product.
- **BGH "Parfumflakon"** (I ZR 256/97, 4 May 2000) [M], restored by the fact-check from the independent note: a distributor who lawfully sells a product may show it in customary advertising, on the strength of exhaustion. This cuts against the quoted "gewisses rechtliches Risiko" for sellers of physical books, whose risk is lower than that sentence suggests. It does not reach eBooks (no exhaustion) and does not reach a newly manufactured printed object.
- **§ 53(1)**: single copies by a natural person for private use, not from an obviously unlawful source; they may be made by a third party if free of charge, or if on paper by photomechanical or similar processes; copies may not be distributed to the public (§ 53(6)); private use includes the close personal circle; for automated services the customer who triggers the process is the maker (BGH "Internet-Videorecorder") — all [M]. On this reading Germany is the most favourable jurisdiction for print-at-home; that ranking is an assessment from memory, with the statute text unread (unverified).
- **§ 57 (unwesentliches Beiwerk)**: only for elements that could be swapped without anyone noticing (BGH "Möbelkatalog") [M]; a cover on the front panel is the opposite.
- **Enforcement**: image Abmahnungen (cease-and-desist with contractual penalty, damages, lawyer's fees) are routine — [IT-Recht Kanzlei FAQ](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/abmahnung-urheberrecht-bilderklau-faq.html) [V1]. Highest practical enforcement risk of the jurisdictions examined.

### 4.4 United Kingdom (CDPA 1988)

- Fair dealing is purpose-bound. Quotation (s.30(1ZA), added 2014): work already public, fair dealing, no more than required, sufficient acknowledgement — [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/30), [2014 Regulations](https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2356/regulation/3/made?view=plain), [UCL](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/copyright/2023/02/24/fair-dealing-week-2023-part-2-three-fair-dealing-exceptions) [V2]. It formally reaches photographs, but a whole image is unlikely to qualify; news reporting excludes photographs (s.30(2)) [V1].
- No private-copying exception: s.28B was quashed in July 2015 [M]. s.63 (advertising the sale of an artistic work) and s.31 (incidental inclusion) do not fit [M]. Published editions also carry a typographical-arrangement right: s.8 defines the published edition and s.15 sets the 25-year term (corrected: the draft cited s.8 for the term) [M].

### 4.5 United States (comparison)

- **Thumbnails are fair use for search and identification**: Kelly v Arriba Soft, 336 F.3d 811 (9th Cir. 2003); Perfect 10 v Amazon (9th Cir. 2007) — [opinion](https://m.openjurist.org/336/f3d/811), [CRS RL33810](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL33810.html) [V2]; library associations supported that position as amici ([EFF brief](https://eff.org/files/filenode/Perfect10_v_Google/effperfect10vgoogleamicus.pdf)) [V1]. LibraryThing states that showing covers for sale, rental or commentary is fair use "in most circumstances" ([LibraryThing blog](https://blog.librarything.com/category/covers/)) [V1] (a site's opinion, not a ruling).
- The reasoning rests on small, low-resolution images serving a different function. A full-size, print-resolution cover on a decorative object sold for money has the same expressive purpose as the original; after Warhol v Goldsmith (2023) that weighs against fair use [M]. This paragraph is analysis: no US decision on a printed cover jacket was found. What is verified is the thumbnail holdings only.
- **First sale** (17 USC § 109) limits only the distribution right in a particular lawful copy and is never a defence to reproduction — [DOJ manual](https://www.justice.gov/usam/title9/crm01854.htm), [Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/?p=1446) [V2].
- **Print-on-demand intermediaries are not reliably "passive"**: the Sixth Circuit reversed a finding that Redbubble was a mere transactional intermediary (OSU v Redbubble, 2021, trade mark) — [Fross Zelnick](https://www.frosszelnick.com/u-s-court-of-appeals-for-the-sixth-circuit-redbubble-could-be-liable-for-direct-infringement-arising-from-print-on-demand-service/), [Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2021/02/ip-lawsuits-against-print-on-demand-vendors-continue-to-vex-the-courts-osu-v-redbubble-more.htm) [V2]; in Australia Redbubble was held to infringe copyright, with nominal damages (Pokémon v Redbubble [2017] FCA 1541, [WILMap](https://wilmap.stanford.edu/node/30497)) [V1]. The picture is not one-sided (added by the fact-check) [M]: Atari v Redbubble and Y.Y.G.M. (Brandy Melville) v Redbubble, both decided by the Ninth Circuit in 2023, went largely Redbubble's way, the second requiring knowledge of specific infringements for contributory liability. US courts are split on print-on-demand intermediaries; the safe reading is that a service which prints and ships cannot count on being treated as passive. No EU judgment on print-on-demand platforms was found.

### 4.6 Other EU Member States

Not researched. Private copying is optional under art. 5(2)(b) and its conditions differ by state (who may make the copy, whether a third party may assist, which categories of work) [M]. The harmonised baseline in §4.1 applies everywhere; before launching print-at-home with user covers in Belgium, the Netherlands, Spain or Italy, the local private-copy text must be read.

The term of protection is harmonised at 70 years after death, with exceptions that matter for the public-domain tier [M]: Spain keeps 80 years for authors who died before 7 December 1987 (transitional rule of its 1987 act); France adds wartime extensions and 30 years for authors "morts pour la France" (CPI L. 123-8 to L. 123-10). See §8.1.

| Architecture | Who makes the copy | Consequence |
|---|---|---|

**How much weight this table bears** (fact-check). Neither decision was read. The EU authorities (VCAST [V2]; Ocilion, Copydan [M]) point the same way, and client-side composition is also the right choice for privacy and performance, so the architecture does not change. ## 5. Moral rights and alterations, including the generated spine

### 5.1 Rules

- Having permission to use a work does not dispense from respecting moral rights — [APIE / economie.gouv](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/node/40472) [V1].
- French case law treats **cropping, mirrored reproduction, colourisation and cut-outs (détourage)** as infringements; a photo may not be cropped, altered or used in a montage without the author's express consent — [Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/pop-et-droit-moral), [practical note](https://www.alsace.catholique.fr/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2016/04/notions-cle-droit-image.pdf) [V2]. The two sources cover those four operations on photographs. **Overprinting a cover and extending artwork onto a spine are not in them**: treating these as infringements is an extrapolation by the authors, reasonable but unsourced. Cropping to fit a cover format: TGI Paris, 5 Oct 2007 ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/juri/id/JURITEXT000018862984)), snippet only, not read (unverified).
- In the "pop" case reported by Livres Hebdo, a reproduction that did not present the work in its integrity, omitted the signature and changed the format cost **EUR 15,000** in damages — [Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/pop-et-droit-moral). One secondary source; the decision was not identified or read (unverified).
- Not automatic: in a Bordeaux judgment of 29 Feb 2024 claimants invoking cropping and a missing credit lost on all claims — [Dalloz](https://www.dalloz-actualite.fr/document/tj-bordeaux-29-fevr-2024-n-2308086). The grounds were not visible, so nothing can be drawn from it (unverified). Courts sanction "abus notoire" of post-mortem moral rights — [Lextenso](https://www.labase-lextenso.fr/l-essentiel-droit-de-la-propriete-intellectuelle/2026-n2/abus-notoire-du-droit-moral-post-mortem-DPI203n9), headline only (unverified).
- Moral rights are perpetual and inalienable (CPI L. 121-1) [M]: a publisher cannot waive them for the illustrator, and a general advance waiver by the author is void. The author can, however, consent to specific, identified modifications [M]. So a publisher opt-in clears the economic right only, and an alteration is cleared only with the artist's own consent to that alteration, which the publisher may collect and warrant.
- Integrity can also be infringed without touching the pixels, by a context that distorts the spirit of the work (French case law on works reused in advertising) [M]. An untouched cover on a tasteful gift card is a weak case for such a claim, but not a null one.
- Elsewhere: Germany protects against distortion that prejudices the author's legitimate interests (UrhG § 14, with § 39 on alterations by licensees) and requires naming (§ 13) [M]; UK integrity right requires prejudice to honour or reputation and can be waived (CDPA ss.80, 87) [M]. France is the strictest of the three.

### 5.3 The 10 mm spine

The card needs a spine the eBook never had. Options, from safest to riskiest:

| Option | Copyright and moral rights | Trade dress | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separate panel, flat neutral colour, title and author retyped in an openly licensed font from our own catalogue | Low. Retyping the title designates the book (referential) | Low if the layout is our own | **Default in every mode** |
| Same, with the panel colour set to one dominant colour measured from the cover | Low (analysis): a single colour value is not a reproduction of the work. No pixels, textures or gradients are taken | Low | Allowed; counsel to confirm |

| Spine reproducing the cover's own title lettering (cut out, traced, or regenerated by an image model) | High: reproduction of part of the cover, a crop, and for hand lettering an adaptation of a graphic work | Medium–High for franchise titles (trade marks) | Not offered without express licence |
| Spine that continues the cover artwork (texture sampled from the edge, mirror, outpainting) | High (§5.2) | — | Not offered without express licence and warranted artist consent |
| Spine imitating a recognisable collection (colours, rules, logo position) | Medium | **High**: parasitisme (§6); EU unregistered design right for looks first shown in the last three years [M] | Not offered |

The font-identification and generative paths are analysed in `notes/legal-fonts-genai.fonts.md` and `notes/legal-fonts-genai.genai.md`; their conclusion matches this table: matching an open look-alike font is acceptable, re-drawing the cover's lettering or outpainting the artwork is the riskiest operation in the product and stays behind a licence flag.

## 6. Trade marks and trade dress

Searches on this section were not run by either researcher, and the fact-check could not run any. Everything is [M] unless marked: **the whole section is unverified** and is a list of points for counsel, not a statement of the law.

- **Registered marks.** Publisher, imprint and collection names and logos ("Folio", "Le Livre de Poche", "Pocket", "10/18") are registered trade marks, typically including class 16 (printed matter). Series and franchise titles are often registered too.
- **Referential use.** A third party may use a mark to designate the genuine product of the proprietor, provided the use follows honest practices and does not suggest a commercial link (Directive (EU) 2015/2436 art. 14(1)(c) and 14(2); CPI L. 713-6; BMW v Deenik, C-63/97). Naming the publisher in plain text, and the logo as part of the unaltered cover, fit this. Dior v Evora adds the limit of serious damage to reputation [V2].
- **Separate logo use.** Lifting the logo out of the cover and placing it on a generated spine or back panel is trade-mark use on a printed product not made by the publisher. It is likely infringement, and it signals "official publisher object" when it is not.
- **Collection trade dress.** A collection's look (Gallimard's "Blanche": cream ground, red and black rules, NRF monogram) can be defended through copyright, figurative marks and **parasitisme** (C. civ. art. 1240). Zulma v Elior, as reported by one law-firm blog, shows parasitisme succeeding for a book cover even where copyright failed (unverified at source, §2). The Cour de cassation's commercial chamber tightened the parasitisme test on 26 June 2024 (the claimant must identify an individualised economic value and an intention to ride on it), per `notes/legal-fonts-genai.fonts.md` [M]. A generated spine that mimics a collection's real spine is riskier than a neutral one.
- **Titles.** An original title is protected (CPI L. 112-4); retyping it to designate the book is referential and low risk.
- **No false origin.** The card should say who made it (reseller or eBook Cadeau) and must not reproduce the book's EAN barcode or present itself as the publisher's product. A card that looks like the book but contains none could also raise a misleading-practice question (C. conso. L. 121-2); a plain "carte cadeau eBook" mention answers it.
- **A platform that presents goods as its own can be the user of the sign** (added by the fact-check). Louboutin v Amazon (C-148/21 and C-184/21, 22 Dec 2022): a marketplace operator may itself be regarded as using a third party's mark where a reasonably observant user would link the operator's own services to the sign, for instance because offers are presented uniformly under the operator's logo and the operator stores and ships. This is the right authority for the point made in §7.3 about cards "rendered under our brand": the more the card looks like an eBook Cadeau product, the more eBook Cadeau answers for every mark printed on it.
- **Design law** (added by the fact-check; detail in §2). A collection's get-up and a recent cover can be protected as an EU design, unregistered for three years against copying, or registered for up to 25 years [sib: M]. This is a second reason, next to parasitisme, not to imitate a collection in a template or a generated spine.
- **Retailer marks in our own material: where the line sits** (rewritten by the fact-check; the draft imposed a blanket ban on retailer names and presented it as a legal rule). "Fnac", "Kobo" and "7switch" are third-party marks; "Fnac" is very likely a mark with a reputation.
  - *Allowed in principle*: naming a retailer in plain text to say what a demo or an integration is modelled on or compatible with. That is the same referential use this document relies on for publisher names (Directive (EU) 2015/2436 art. 14(1)(c); CPI L. 713-6). Conditions: honest practices, no more of the mark than needed (the word, not the logo), no suggestion of partnership or endorsement, and a short no-affiliation notice.
  - *Exposed*: reproducing a retailer's logo, colours, page layout or tone so that the demo looks like its site (trade-mark infringement, parasitisme, and copyright in the site design); a look-alike or parody name close enough to call the mark to mind, such as a one-letter variant (imitation with a likelihood of confusion, CPI L. 713-2; for a mark with a reputation, taking unfair advantage without due cause, L. 713-3). French trade-mark law has no general parody defence for commercial uses.
  - *Consequence for the project*: the brief asks for "a fake 7switch" and "a fake Fnac". The demos reproduce the business archetype of each (an independent store selling watermarked files; a large multi-product retailer delivering into customer accounts) under invented names and an original design. The repository's "Septante" and "Grande Enseigne" stores are of this kind. Documentation may state which real retailer each archetype is modelled on. An invented, clearly different name is safer than a near-miss of the real one.
  - The rule for covers is unchanged and is a copyright rule, not a trade-mark one: fixtures and marketing use invented titles, public-domain artwork or covers licensed by a partner.
- "QR Code" is a registered word mark of Denso Wave; the usual practice is an acknowledgement line where the term is printed.

## 7. Liability, takedown, warranties, insurance

### 7.1 Who is exposed

| Actor | Role | Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Reseller (mode R) | Principal: decides to offer the card, supplies the cover | Contrefaçon (civil and criminal), breach of its distribution contract, loss of the catalogue feed |
| eBook Cadeau, composing on a server or ordering the print | Reproduces and distributes | Co-infringer; no hosting exemption for its own acts (§7.3) |
| eBook Cadeau, supplying a browser tool only | Tool supplier | Mainly indirect theories; primary liability is possible if the tool is designed or marketed for reproducing third-party covers (§4.7) |
| Printer | Reproduces | Co-infringer in principle; printers' terms usually pass the risk back to the customer through a rights warranty [M] |
| End user printing on a printer of their own | Arguably the copier | Private-copy argument; practically never pursued |
| Copy shop, kiosk or employer whose printer the user borrows | Copier under Rannou-Graphie [M] | No private-copy exception; in practice never pursued for a single print, but it defeats the user's argument (§4.7) |
| Publisher opting in | Licensor | Liable to its illustrator or photographer if it grants more than it holds (§2) |

Good faith is no defence to civil infringement in France [M]. Contrefaçon is also a délit (CPI L. 335-2: up to 3 years and EUR 300,000 [M]). Damages take into account, separately, the claimant's economic loss, its moral prejudice and the infringer's profits, including the investments the infringer saved. Alternatively, at the claimant's request, the court may award a lump sum, which must be **higher than** the royalties that would have been due had authorisation been sought, and which does not exclude compensation for moral prejudice (CPI L. 331-1-3, as amended by loi n° 2014-315 of 11 March 2014) [M]. (Corrected: the draft gave the pre-2014 formula, "at least equal to the fee". The practical effect is that the evaded licence fee is a floor that is always exceeded, not the measure.) Reference amounts found, each from one secondary source and with the decision unread (unverified): EUR 15,000 in the "pop" case; three times the evaded licence fee (TJ Bordeaux, 26 June 2025). In Germany the usual instrument is the Abmahnung [V1]. For a reseller the realistic commercial risk is less a damages award than a publisher or distributor suspending the feed.

Cross-border: infringement is judged under the law of the country for which protection is claimed (Rome II Regulation, art. 8(1) [M]). Shipping a card to Germany or Belgium brings that country's law and courts.

### 7.2 Regulatory side-issues

- Prime rule and fixed price (§3.1) [M]: a free printed and mailed card is the exposed case; a free print-at-home PDF probably is not a prime; a paid optional card is not. The médiateur du livre can be asked.
- VAT (§3.1) [M]: conservative position, a paid optional card is a distinct supply at the standard rate; the voucher treatment of the code depends on whether redemption is limited to one Member State.
- Misleading presentation and false origin (§6) [M].

### 7.3 Intermediary status (DSA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2065)

- Art. 6 keeps the hosting exemption for providers that store information at the user's request, have no actual knowledge and act expeditiously once aware. It is lost where the provider plays an active role giving it knowledge or control (the line from Google France and L'Oréal v eBay); injunctions remain possible (art. 6(4)). Art. 6(3) lifts the exemption only for liability **under consumer-protection law**, and only for online platforms that let consumers conclude distance contracts with traders, where the platform presents the item or the transaction so that an average consumer would believe it comes from the platform itself or from someone under its control [M on the text]. It is not an intellectual-property rule (corrected: the draft read it as one). Sources for the article as a whole, [V2] on its existence and general content — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/the-digital-services-act-new-liability-rules-102iiyf), [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/fr/interface/2022/the-eus-digital-services-act/online-intermediaries-and-illegal-content-under-the-digital-services-act), [BEUC](https://www.beuc.eu/sites/default/files/publications/ConsumerPro_DSA-complement_to_TBD_2023.pdf), [Busch report](https://digitalplanet.tufts.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/DD-Report_2-Christoph-Busch-11.30.22.pdf).
- **Where hosting status helps and where it does not.** It can cover a user-uploaded file stored at the user's request. It cannot cover (i) covers the service selects or fetches by ISBN (own content), (ii) printing and shipping, where the service manufactures the object (Redbubble analogy), (iii) anything rendered under the service's brand as its own product. For (iii) the authority is not DSA art. 6(3), which is only an analogy from consumer law, but the active-role case law: for trade marks, Louboutin v Amazon (C-148/21 and C-184/21, §6); for copyright, the line from L'Oréal v eBay to YouTube/Cyando (C-682/18 and C-683/18) [M]. Both notes agree on the three limits. Client-side composition avoids the question: nothing is hosted.
- France: LCEN (loi 2004-575, art. 6), aligned on the DSA since the 2024 "SREN" law [M].
- US: DMCA § 512 requires a registered agent and a repeat-infringer policy [M]; many print-on-demand sites follow it ([IPWatchdog](https://www.ipwatchdog.com/2021/03/09/litigation-threatening-burst-redbubble-courts-weigh-ip-implications-redbubbles-unique-online-marketplace/id=130712/)) [V1].

### 7.4 Notice and takedown

- Art. 16 DSA: every hosting provider needs an electronic notice mechanism; a sufficiently precise notice gives actual knowledge; decisions must be timely, diligent, non-arbitrary and objective — [Burges Salmon](https://burges-salmon.com/news-and-insight/legal-updates/data-protection/the-eu-digital-services-act-and-the-article-6-hosting-exemption), [art. 16 text](https://www.springlex.eu/fr/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-16/) [V2]. Contact points (arts. 11–12), statement of reasons (art. 17), small-enterprise carve-out from platform-only duties (art. 19) [M].
- **Delay.** The reseller notes propose 24–48 h (24 business hours in the draft clause); the independent notes propose same-day removal. Kept: a contractual commitment of 24 business hours, an operating target of the same business day.
- **Scope of a takedown.** Stop new generation for the ISBN (or the publisher's whole ISBN prefix on request); purge cached covers; regenerate unprinted orders as typographic cards; no recall of cards already delivered (sell-off rule to be agreed in the contract).

### 7.5 Warranty chain

Rights and warranties must be back-to-back: publisher → reseller (clause in §3.8) → eBook Cadeau (article below) → printer (the printer's terms). An indemnity shifts cost, not liability: rights holders can still sue eBook Cadeau directly; it is worth the reseller's solvency; and a party that knowingly took part in an infringement may be refused the benefit of the warranty [M].

Draft for counsel, in French (operative language for French contracts):

> **Article X — Contenus Éditoriaux ; garantie**
>
> **X.1** « Contenus Éditoriaux » désigne, pour chaque Titre, la première de couverture, les textes de présentation, titre, nom(s) d'auteur, mentions de crédit et tout autre élément transmis par le Revendeur.
>
> **X.2 Déclarations du Revendeur.** Pour chaque Titre transmis avec l'indicateur « impression de la couverture autorisée », le Revendeur déclare et garantit : (a) détenir de l'éditeur ou de son distributeur une autorisation écrite couvrant la reproduction des Contenus Éditoriaux sur un support imprimé ou imprimable remis à l'acheteur d'un exemplaire numérique du Titre, y compris par l'intermédiaire de sous-traitants ; (b) que cette autorisation couvre le territoire de livraison, la durée du Contrat et une Carte par exemplaire numérique vendu ; (c) que les mentions de crédit transmises sont complètes ; (d) n'avoir connaissance d'aucune restriction de tiers (visuel sous licence limitée, affiche de film, personnage sous licence) non couverte par cette autorisation ; (e) que, lorsque l'œuvre reproduite en couverture relève du répertoire d'un organisme de gestion collective (notamment l'ADAGP ou la SAIF), l'autorisation de cet organisme a été obtenue pour cet usage, ou que le Titre n'est pas transmis avec l'indicateur.
>
> **X.3 Sous-licence.** Le Revendeur concède au Prestataire, pour la seule exécution du Service et pour la durée du Contrat, une sous-licence non exclusive et non cessible de reproduction des Contenus Éditoriaux, que le Prestataire peut exercer par l'intermédiaire des imprimeurs listés en Annexe.
>
> **X.4 Engagements du Prestataire.** Le Prestataire s'engage à : (a) reproduire la couverture sans recadrage, surimpression ni modification autre que la mise à l'échelle proportionnelle et la conversion colorimétrique nécessaires à l'impression ; (b) faire figurer les mentions de crédit transmises ; (c) composer le dos et les autres volets avec ses propres éléments typographiques, hors de l'image de couverture, et ne reproduire isolément aucune marque ou logo de l'éditeur sans autorisation expresse ; (d) cesser toute génération pour un Titre dans les [24] heures ouvrées suivant notification de retrait ; (e) tenir un journal des Cartes générées par ISBN.
>
> **X.5 Garantie.** Le Revendeur garantit le Prestataire contre toute réclamation ou action d'un tiers (éditeur, auteur, illustrateur, photographe, agence, titulaire de marque, organisme de gestion collective) alléguant que la reproduction des Contenus Éditoriaux conformément au Contrat porte atteinte à un droit de propriété intellectuelle ou de la personnalité, ou constitue un acte de concurrence déloyale ou parasitaire. Il prend en charge les condamnations, les indemnités transactionnelles qu'il a approuvées, les frais et honoraires raisonnables de défense et les coûts de retrait. Le Prestataire notifie la réclamation sous [10] jours ouvrés, coopère à la défense et ne transige pas sans l'accord du Revendeur.
>
> **X.6 Exclusions et réciprocité.** La garantie ne couvre pas les réclamations résultant (i) d'un manquement du Prestataire à l'article X.4, (ii) des éléments fournis par le Prestataire (polices, fonds, éléments générés, logiciels), pour lesquels le Prestataire garantit le Revendeur dans les mêmes termes, (iii) d'une génération postérieure au délai de retrait.
>
> **X.7 Plafond.** Les limitations de responsabilité de l'article [●] ne s'appliquent pas au présent article [variante : plafond spécifique de [●] € par sinistre]. Chaque partie justifie d'une assurance responsabilité civile professionnelle couvrant la contrefaçon.
>
> **X.8 Repli.** En l'absence de l'indicateur visé en X.2, le Prestataire génère une Carte typographique ne reproduisant ni la couverture ni aucune marque figurative.

English gist: the reseller warrants written publisher authorisation for print through subcontractors, and clearance from the collective management organisation where the artwork is in its repertoire (X.2; point (e) was added by the fact-check); narrow sub-licence (X.3); the service commits to integrity, credits, an own-material spine, 24 h takedown and a per-ISBN log (X.4); the reseller indemnifies against third-party IP, personality and unfair-competition claims (X.5); carve-outs and a reciprocal indemnity for what the service adds, i.e. fonts and generated spines (X.6); indemnity outside the general cap, plus insurance (X.7); text-only fallback (X.8). Between professionals, limitation clauses are valid unless they void an essential obligation (C. civ. art. 1170) [M].

**User terms (standalone).** A declaration of rights or of private use, one print per gift code, printing on the user's own printer, no resale. This supports the private-copy narrative and gives a contractual basis to refuse service. As an indemnity against a consumer it is weak, and it may be worse than weak: a clause making a consumer warrant third-party rights and indemnify the professional can be struck out as an unfair term creating a significant imbalance (C. conso. L. 212-1) [M]. The consumer-facing text is therefore drafted as a declaration and a condition of use, not as an indemnity; the warranty-and-indemnity wording is kept for professional orderers (authors, publishers, resellers).

### 7.6 Insurance

Not researched; all [M] and unverified. General professional-liability (RC pro) policies often exclude intellectual-property infringement or cover it only through a specific extension (media or technology errors-and-omissions cover). Intentional fault is uninsurable (C. assur. L. 113-1) and criminal fines cannot be insured. Action: ask a broker for a quote that names "atteinte aux droits de propriété intellectuelle" for a print-on-demand composition service, with defence costs, and ask whether cover is conditional on a rights-clearance procedure (the flags and logs in the last section are that procedure). Clause X.7 requires the reseller to hold equivalent cover.

## 8. Recommended product policy and safe fallbacks

### 8.1 Tiers

| Tier | What it is | Where it may be used | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T0 — Typographic card** | Title, author, publisher name as text; our templates; open fonts; no logos | Everywhere: home print, professional print, reseller mode | None. This is the default and the universal fallback |
| **T1 — Licensed cover** | Unaltered cover on the front panel, credit line printed | Home print and professional print, reseller mode | Per-title flag backed by a written publisher authorisation; reseller warranty (art. X) |
| **T1r — Reduced-size cover** | Unaltered cover at reduced size on a neutral ground | Reseller mode | Reseller's written declaration that its licence covers promotion on print media; disabled by default |
| **T2 — Rights-holder cover** | Cover supplied by the author or publisher ordering their own cards | Home print and professional print, standalone | Declaration and warranty by the orderer (the Juniper Custom rule); stock-licence reminder |
| **T3 — User-supplied cover, private** | Cover file picked by the user in the browser | Home print only, standalone, on the user's own printer | Client-side only; file never reaches our servers; the tool never decrypts a protected file; one card per gift code; user confirms private, non-commercial use and printing on their own printer. Enabled in France only after counsel's opinion (rule 11) |
| **T4 — Public-domain artwork** | Period or first-edition cover whose term of protection has expired | In each country where the per-country test below passes (home print: the user's country; printed card: the country of delivery as well) | Death year of every author of the artwork and first-publication year recorded; source without contractual restriction; no distortion |

**Per-country test for T4** (corrected by the fact-check: the draft applied "artist dead more than 70 years" everywhere, which is wrong outside France and most of the EU). All [M]; each line must be read at source before launch in that country.

| Country | Test | Note |
|---|---|---|
| France | Current year minus death year of the last surviving author is at least 71 | Plus wartime extensions and 30 years for authors "morts pour la France" (CPI L. 123-8 to L. 123-10): the interface warns and asks |
| Germany and most EU Member States | Same 71-year test, last surviving author | Harmonised term (Directive 2006/116) |
| Spain | At least 81 years if the author died before 7 December 1987; otherwise 71 | Transitional rule keeping the earlier 80-year term |
| United Kingdom | Same 71-year test for most artistic works | Edge cases (Crown copyright, some unpublished works) are not handled: refuse when unsure |
| United States | First published at least 96 years before the current year (before 1931, as of 2026) | The term of older works runs from publication, not from death. A cover first published in 1931 or later can still be protected, whatever the artist's death date |
| Any other country | Not offered automatically | Typographic card instead |

A cover with several authors (illustrator and letterer, for instance) is counted from the death of the last survivor. Where a death year or a publication year is unknown, the test fails. A faithful photograph of a public-domain two-dimensional work gains no new right in the EU (DSM Directive art. 14 [M]); the host's contractual terms still apply.

### 8.2 Rules

1. **Default output is T0** in every mode and on every print route.
2. **Artwork on a professionally printed card requires T1, T2 or T4.** No professional printing of unlicensed covers.
3. **The service never fetches a cover into a printable artefact** in standalone mode. ISBN lookup returns text metadata. A thumbnail may be shown in the UI only from a source whose terms allow it, and it is not embedded in the PDF.
4. **Covers are never modified** (§5.2). The 10 mm spine is typeset (§5.3).
5. **Tie-in, licensed-character, fine-art and rights-managed covers are excluded** unless the publisher clears the title specifically.
6. **No separate use of logos, blurbs, extracts or author photographs** without a per-title flag.
7. **Public-domain titles**: the cover is a separate work; offer T4 only where the per-country test of §8.1 passes for the country of use and, for a printed card, the country of delivery.
8. **Takedown**: public notice form, same-business-day target, ISBN and ISBN-prefix blocklist, log of notices and decisions.
9. **UK and US users**: same policy for tiers T0 to T3; nothing printed relies on fair use or fair dealing. T4 is the exception: the public-domain test is different in each country (§8.1), and T3 has no statutory basis in the UK.
10. **Card pricing in reseller mode**: a printed card is a separate, optional, separately invoiced line, at or above cost, until the prime question is cleared by counsel or by the médiateur du livre. A free print-at-home PDF may be offered. A free printed card needs the publisher to offer it to all retailers.
11. **Written opinion from French IP counsel** before enabling T1r, T3 in France, or any professional printing of third-party covers.
12. **"Home print" means the user's own printer.** The interface says so wherever a cover-bearing PDF is produced under T3, and does not suggest copy shops, photo kiosks or print services for that file.
13. **No decryption.** The tool reads a cover only from an image file or from an eBook file that carries no encryption. It never removes or bypasses a protection measure and never explains how to.
14. **Demo and marketing material**: invented store brands and an original design; real retailers named only descriptively, with a no-affiliation notice (§6).

### 8.3 Mitigations and their cost

| Mitigation | Effect | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Client-side (WASM) composition, no server copy of user covers | Keeps the best available argument that the user is the copier (not a proven shield, §4.7); removes the hosting question | Already the architecture |
| Ask the médiateur du livre, or counsel, whether a free card is a prime | Settles the pricing rule for reseller mode | Low; slow |
| Per-country public-domain gate; own-printer notice; no-decryption rule | Removes three errors of the draft policy | Small amount of interface and validation work |
| Typographic card as default and fallback | Removes third-party artwork entirely | Template and font work |

| Opt-out blocklist by ISBN prefix, honoured quickly | Limits exposure with litigious rights holders | Low |
| Notice-and-action process (art. 16 form, logged decisions, contact point; DMCA agent if US traffic) | Preserves the safe harbour where it applies; shows good faith | Low |

| No scraping of retailer or aggregator sites for print assets | Avoids Renckhoff, database-right and contract claims | Fewer covers in preview |

## 9. Questions for counsel

**Reseller mode**

1. Does a licence to use the cover "pour la promotion et la vente", without media wording, cover a printed card handed to the buyer of the eBook? The licence is between professionals, so the question is one of ordinary interpretation (C. civ. arts. 1188 to 1190), CPI L. 131-3 applying only to the author's own contract upstream (Cass. 1re civ., 13 Oct 1993, "Perrier"): is that reading right? Does "sur tous supports" change the answer? Does reduced size? Does art. 1190 help a reseller bound by a distributor's standard terms?
2. Is a card given free with an eBook a "vente à prime" under art. 4 of loi 2011-590? Does the answer differ for a free print-at-home PDF and for a free printed and mailed card? Do the packaging, delivery-vehicle or low-value arguments apply? Is a card sold below cost a disguised discount on the fixed price? Is a referral to the médiateur du livre advisable? What VAT rate applies to a paid optional card, can it be argued ancillary, and does bundling affect the eBook rate? Is the code a single-purpose voucher when redemption is limited to France?
3. Under a contrat de mandat, may the retailer launch this format without the publisher's instruction (C. civ. art. 1989)?
4. For Fnac-type chains (publisher → distributor → Kobo → Fnac → service → printer), which link must grant the sub-licence, and can the reseller's warranty (art. X) be relied on if one link is missing?
5. Is a publisher's opt-in sufficient where the cover uses a commissioned illustration (Cass. 2 July 2014) or a stock image? Where the artist is a member of ADAGP or SAIF, who licenses a printed gift card: the publisher or the society? Is a paid card "merchandise" under standard stock licences? What wording should the publisher's warranty carry?
6. What do Dilicom/FEL and Electre licences allow for covers obtained through them?

**Standalone mode**

7. With composition entirely in the user's browser, is the user the "copiste"? Rannou-Graphie designates the party that holds the equipment and exploits it by making it available to customers: can a supplier of browser software be that party? Do Wizzgo (TGI Paris, 25 Nov 2008; CA Paris, 14 Dec 2011) and Ocilion (C-426/21) change the answer? Is the argument lost when the user prints the PDF at a copy shop or at work, and does the reprography regime (CPI L. 122-10 ff.) then apply? Is a single card given to a friend "usage privé du copiste"? Does the art-work carve-out in L. 122-5 2° ("fins identiques") apply to a cover reproduced as a cover?
8. What is the service's exposure as tool supplier (C. pén. art. 121-7; C. civ. art. 1240), can it become primary liability under the Filmspeler and Ziggo reasoning, and which design or marketing choices raise it?
9. For professional printing ordered by a self-declared rights holder, is a declaration plus warranty adequate diligence, or is some verification expected? Does the DSA hosting exemption apply to any part of that flow?
10. In Germany, could a single paid paper copy ordered by a consumer fall under § 53(1) sentence 2 UrhG, and what is the Abmahnung exposure for cards shipped to Germany?

**Moral rights, spine, marks**

11. Is an untouched cover placed next to a generated spine and back panel an infringement of integrity by context? Is inset placement with a neutral frame safer than full bleed?
12. Is setting the spine colour to a dominant colour measured from the cover free of any reproduction issue?
13. May the publisher's logo appear as part of the unaltered cover on an object not made by the publisher (referential use, class 16)? Should the card carry an "objet non édité par l'éditeur" line?
14. Can a purely typographic card that takes the form of a book jacket for a named title be attacked as parasitisme after the June 2024 Cour de cassation decisions? Does L. 112-4 (titles) add anything?
15. What credit line should be printed when the feed supplies none?

**Liability and operations**

16. Are clauses X.5 and X.7 enforceable as drafted, and what cap is market practice? What insurance wording is needed?
17. What takedown delay and sell-off rule are reasonable, and how long should generation logs be kept (civil and criminal limitation)?
18. Criminal exposure of the directors and of the printer; what the printer's terms should say.
19. Public-domain artwork: reliance on faithful reproductions of two-dimensional works (DSM Directive art. 14 [M]) and on host terms; "mort pour la France" and wartime extensions; is the per-country table of §8.1 correct, and which country's term governs a card composed in France and delivered abroad?

**Added by the fact-check**

20. Reading a cover from the user's own eBook file: is it lawful for an unprotected or watermarked EPUB? Is reading an unencrypted cover image inside a protected EPUB a circumvention (CPI L. 331-5 ff.)? Do retailers' terms of use forbid reusing a cover saved from a product page?
21. EU unregistered design right: can a recent cover or a collection's get-up be enforced against a card that reproduces it (not only against one that imitates it)? Does the private, non-commercial exemption cover the home-print user?
22. Is a rights declaration imposed on a consumer, with an indemnity, an unfair term (C. conso. L. 212-1)? What wording is safe?
23. Demo stores and documentation: is a descriptive mention of "Fnac" or "7switch" ("modelled on", "compatible with") with a no-affiliation notice within CPI L. 713-6? Is an allusive invented name acceptable?
24. Under the corrected CPI L. 331-1-3 (lump sum higher than the royalties due, plus moral prejudice), what order of magnitude should be budgeted per infringing title for an automated service?

## Implications for the implementation

Numbers marked (proposal) are design choices derived from the analysis, not legal thresholds; counsel should confirm them.

Items 1 to 36 keep the numbers of the draft, because sibling documents cite them. The fact-check changed items 3, 14, 19, 20, 21, 22, 24, 28, 29 and 36 in place and added items 37 to 43. None of the legal points behind these items was verified at source (see the Verification log); they are conservative defaults, to be confirmed by counsel.

**Rights model**

1. Every cover asset carries a mandatory rights basis with exactly these values: `none` (default), `print_licence` (T1), `promo_all_media` (T1r), `rights_holder_declared` (T2), `user_private_copy` (T3), `public_domain` (T4). No code path may place a raster or vector cover in a print file when the basis is `none`.
2. The default output of every entry point (web, command line, reseller API) is the typographic card: title, subtitle, author names, publisher name and ISBN as plain text, set in fonts from the project's own openly licensed catalogue. No logo, no blurb, no barcode.
3. Print-route gate: home-print PDF accepts every basis except `none`; a professional print order accepts only `print_licence`, `rights_holder_declared` and `public_domain`; `promo_all_media` yields the reduced-size layout only; `user_private_copy` never reaches a print order. `public_domain` passes a route only for the countries in which the test of item 22 passes.
4. The command-line and self-hosted tools require an explicit rights-basis argument to embed a cover and print a one-line notice that the operator is responsible for the rights; without the argument they produce the typographic card.

**Cover integrity (enforced in layout and again in preflight)**

5. A cover is placed with uniform scaling only: horizontal and vertical scale factors equal within 0.1 %; rotation 0°; the clip box equals the image box (no crop); no colour change other than ICC conversion to the output profile.
6. Aspect-ratio mismatch is resolved by letterboxing on a flat neutral ground, never by cropping or stretching.
7. No object may intersect the cover's bounding box: QR code, redemption code, ribbons, text, fold marks and crop marks are placed on other panels or outside the trim. Preflight fails the file otherwise.
8. Bleed is 3 mm and is filled with the flat ground colour, not with mirrored or extended artwork. By default the cover is inset at least 3 mm inside the trim on its outer edges so that trimming tolerance never cuts the artwork. Full-bleed placement (trimming up to 3 mm of artwork) is allowed only when the title carries `trim_authorised = true`.
9. Reduced-size layout (`promo_all_media`): the cover occupies at most 60 % of the front panel's width and height (at most 36 % of its area) (proposal), on a neutral ground, with the title retyped beside it.
10. When a cover is printed, a credit line is printed on the back or inside panel at 6 pt minimum (proposal): the feed's credit text when supplied (ONIX List 160 codes 01 and 03), otherwise "Couverture : © {publisher}" with `credit_missing = true` recorded in the generation log.

**Spine (10 mm) and other panels**

11. The spine is always a separate panel. Default: flat colour, title and author typeset from metadata text in an open font. The colour may be a single dominant colour computed from the cover; no pixels, textures or gradients are sampled from the cover.
12. Outpainting, mirroring or stretching the artwork onto the spine, cutting out or regenerating the cover's title lettering, and any image-model output derived from the cover are disabled unless the title carries `adaptation_authorised = true` and the basis is `print_licence` or `rights_holder_declared`. The flag defaults to false.
13. A publisher-supplied spine or flat full-cover file is used as supplied with uniform scaling. Re-fitting it to 10 mm requires `adaptation_authorised = true`; otherwise the typeset spine is used.
14. Templates are original designs. No template may reproduce a publisher's collection layout or the look of an existing cover (parasitisme; EU design right, including the unregistered right for designs less than three years old); no template takes a publisher logo. Publisher, imprint and collection logos are accepted only inside the unaltered cover image, or as separate elements when `logo_use = true`.
15. Blurbs, extracts, review quotes and author photographs are printed only when the corresponding per-title flag is true (`blurb_use`, `extract_use`, `author_photo_use`); all default to false.
16. Every card carries an imprint line naming who produced it (reseller or eBook Cadeau) and the mention "carte cadeau eBook" in the card's language. The book's EAN barcode is never reproduced. Where "QR Code" is printed as a term, the Denso Wave acknowledgement line is added.

**Standalone mode**

17. A cover enters the standalone flow only through a local file picked or dropped in the browser. Decoding, analysis and PDF composition run client-side in WebAssembly. There is no upload endpoint for cover images in the home-print flow, and an integration test asserts that no network request carries the image bytes.
18. The service has no server-side proxy or fetcher that inserts a cover into a PDF. ISBN lookup returns text metadata only.
19. A preview thumbnail of a cover the service has no rights in is at most 400 px on its long edge (proposal), is hotlinked by the browser from an allow-listed source, is not cached or re-served by our servers, is not read into a canvas, and is never embedded in the PDF. A source is allow-listed only if it shows the cover with the rights holder's consent, its terms allow display, and it applies no anti-hotlinking measure; the browser never works around a referrer check, a signed URL or any other such measure, and a failed load shows a placeholder. Covers found by the acquisition tools are stored as `none` with their source URL and the date the source's terms were last read.
20. On cover import the user must choose one declaration, stored with the session: "I am the author or publisher, or hold a licence" (`rights_holder_declared`); "copy for my private use, from a book I bought, printed on my own printer" (`user_private_copy`); "the artwork is in the public domain" (`public_domain`, requires the data of item 22). No choice means no cover.
21. `user_private_copy` produces exactly one jacket per gift code: one jacket per PDF, no multi-up imposition, no professional-print button, no link or hint to a copy shop, photo kiosk or print service, and the generation counter for that code blocks a second cover-bearing PDF. The download screen states that the file is for the user's own printer.
22. `public_domain` is a per-country gate, not one rule. Inputs: the death year of every author of the artwork (the latest one counts), the year of first publication, the user's country and, for a print order, the delivery country. With Y = current year: France, Germany and other EU Member States except Spain: Y minus death year at least 71. Spain: at least 81 if the death was before 7 December 1987, otherwise 71. United Kingdom: at least 71. United States: Y minus first-publication year at least 96 (published before 1931, as of 2026); the death year is ignored. Any other country, or any missing input: refused, typographic card. For France the interface warns about wartime extensions and authors "morts pour la France" and asks the user to confirm that neither applies. The thresholds are stored as data, with the date they were last checked, so that counsel's corrections need no code change.
23. A professional print order with `rights_holder_declared` requires an account, the declarant's name, role and email, and acceptance of the rights warranty; the declaration is logged with the order.

**Reseller API**

24. Per-title fields, all defaulting to the restrictive value: `cover_rights` (`none`, `promo_all_media`, `print_licence`), `adaptation_authorised`, `trim_authorised`, `logo_use`, `blurb_use`, `extract_use`, `author_photo_use`, `credit_line`, `excluded_reason` (`tie_in`, `licensed_character`, `rights_managed`, `fine_art`), `cleared_per_title`, `territories` (ISO country list for delivery), `licence_reference`, `valid_until`. When the reseller's data comes from an ONIX feed, `territories` is filled from the `<Territory>` of the supporting resource and `valid_until` from its `<ContentDate>` with role 15 "Until date" (role 14 "From date" is honoured as an embargo); a resource with `<ContentAudience>` 01 "Restricted" is never used.
25. A title with an `excluded_reason`, or flagged as a media tie-in edition in ONIX, is forced to the typographic card unless `cleared_per_title = true`.
26. When `cover_rights = none` the API ignores any image payload and does not store it. Cover files received under a licence are cached for the life of the order plus 30 days (proposal) and purged on takedown.
27. A cover-bearing card is not shipped to a country outside the title's `territories`; the order falls back to the typographic card.
28. `promo_all_media` is disabled per reseller until the reseller's written declaration is recorded in its account configuration. `print_licence` is disabled per reseller until its acceptance of the warranty (article X, including point X.2(e) on artwork under collective management) is recorded there.
29. In reseller checkout flows (including the demo stores) a **printed** card is an optional line item, never pre-ticked, with its own price and the standard VAT rate (20 % in France); a price of zero for a printed card is rejected unless the reseller configuration carries `publisher_premium = true`. A **print-at-home PDF** may be offered at no charge and adds no line. The eBook line keeps its own price and rate; the two are never merged into one bundle price. The VAT treatment of the code itself depends on its redemption territory and is specified in `09-legal-gifting-and-commerce.md`.

**Takedown, logging, metadata**

30. A blocklist keyed by ISBN and by ISBN registrant prefix is checked at every generation: enforced server-side for reseller and print orders, and fetched as a signed list by the browser for home print. A blocked title yields the typographic card.
31. Takedown: new generation stops within 24 business hours of a valid notice (target: same business day); cached covers are purged; unprinted orders are regenerated as typographic cards; delivered cards are not recalled.
32. A public notice form collects the DSA art. 16 elements (reasons, ISBN or URL, notifier's name and email, good-faith statement), acknowledges receipt, and records the decision with its reasons. A contact point is published.
33. Server-side generations are logged per card: ISBN, rights basis, flags, reseller identifier, licence reference, template, printer, timestamp. Retention: 6 years (proposal, to cover civil and criminal limitation). Client-side home-print generations log nothing about the cover.
34. Each PDF embeds in its XMP metadata the rights basis, credit line, licence reference and generator version, so that a printed card can be traced to its authorisation.

**Tests and fixtures**

35. Integration tests assert the gates: a reseller feed with `cover_rights = none` yields a PDF with no image on the front panel; a crop, overlay or non-uniform scale fails preflight; a blocked ISBN yields the typographic card; a `user_private_copy` session cannot create a print order.
36. Demo stores, fixtures and screenshots use invented retailer brands with an original design, and invented or public-domain books with own-made or public-domain covers. No real retailer or publisher logo, colour scheme, page layout or look-alike name (a near-miss spelling of a real brand), and no real in-copyright cover, is committed to the repository. A real retailer may be named in plain text in documentation to say which archetype a demo is modelled on ("a store of the 7switch type", "a retailer of the Fnac type"), with a line stating that the project is not affiliated with it; the demo's user interface itself shows only the invented brand. Public-domain covers in fixtures pass the gate of item 22 for every country in which the demo is published.

**Added by the fact-check**

37. No decryption. The cover of an eBook file is read only when the package carries no encryption: for an EPUB, no `META-INF/encryption.xml` entry other than font obfuscation, and no `rights.xml` or licence document. If the package is encrypted in any way the tool reads nothing from it, says why, and offers an image-file picker or the typographic card. No code path removes, bypasses or keys a protection scheme, and the interface gives no instruction for doing so. (The stricter of two readings is chosen until counsel answers question 20.)
38. Own printer. Every screen that delivers a PDF produced under `user_private_copy` carries the sentence that it is to be printed on the user's own printer. A typographic card, and a card produced under any other rights basis, carries no such restriction and may be taken to any printer.
39. Consumer wording. The standalone declaration of item 20 is a condition of use, not an indemnity: no clause makes a consumer indemnify the service. The warranty-and-indemnity text of item 23 is shown only to professional orderers.
40. Country inputs. The standalone flow records the user's country (chosen, not inferred silently) and, for a print order, the delivery country; both feed items 22 and 27. A card is not generated under `public_domain` or `user_private_copy` for a country where the corresponding rule is not implemented. `user_private_copy` is unavailable in the United Kingdom, which has no private-copy exception (proposal).
41. ONIX ingestion. A publisher-supplied flat cover (List 158 code 29) or spine image (code 56) is accepted as the "real spine" of item 13; a cover holding image (code 45) is never printed.
42. Marketing and interface copy never present the tool as a way to print a book's cover. The landing page, the store listings and the default flow show the typographic card; the cover options appear only after a rights basis is chosen. An automated check fails the build if marketing strings match a deny-list of such phrasings (proposal).
43. Feature switches. `user_private_copy` (T3), `promo_all_media` (T1r) and free printed cards are behind per-country configuration switches, off by default. A country is switched on once its rule has been read at source and the reading is recorded in the configuration; for France this requires counsel's written opinion (rule 11 of §8.2). The demo environment may switch them on, with invented titles and own-made covers only.

### Effect on conclusions

The product policy stands: typographic card by default, covers only with a recorded rights basis, no alteration, typeset spine. Five things changed.
