## A. Load-bearing claims (20, plus 3 extra)

1. **Loi 2011-590 art. 4: a free card "may be a vente à prime"** (§1.1-9, §3.1). UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees on the article wording [MEM-M]. PARTLY on the consequence.
   - The document never tests whether the card is a "prime" at all. The sibling note distinguishes a free print-at-home PDF (low risk), a free printed and mailed card (medium-high) and a paid optional card (not a prime) [REPO: `legal-gifting-commerce.commerce.md` lines 50-52].
   - The classic carve-outs for usual packaging, items indispensable to normal use and low-value items (former C. conso. R. 121-8 to R. 121-10) are not considered [MEM-M; current status unknown].
   - Corrected: "A free printed and mailed card is plausibly a prime; a free print-at-home PDF probably is not; the delivery-vehicle/packaging argument is untested."

2. **VAT: the card "is a separate supply at the standard rate"; a single-title code "is a single-purpose voucher"** (§1.1-9, §3.1). PARTLY [REPO].
   - The sibling note says separate pricing is "a strong indicator, not conclusive", a free PDF is ancillary, and a taxpayer could argue a paid card is ancillary (line 107).
   - It also says the code is single-purpose only if redemption is limited to one Member State; an EU-wide code is arguably multi-purpose (lines 14, 119-125).
   - eBook 5.5 % and standard 20 %: memory agrees [MEM-H].
   - Corrected: "Conservative position: an optional, separately priced card is a distinct supply at 20 %; single-purpose voucher only for France-only redemption."

3. **CPI L. 131-3 makes the reseller's licence narrow** (§1.1-1, §3.2, §3.5, §4.2, Q1). WRONG as to the authority [MEM-H]; the conclusion probably survives.
   - L. 131-3 formalism protects the author in contracts the author signs. The Cour de cassation held it does not govern contracts between an assignee and sub-exploiters (Cass. 1re civ., 13 Oct 1993, "Perrier"; pourvoi number to be checked).
   - A publisher → distributor → reseller licence is read under ordinary contract law (C. civ. arts. 1188 ff.). The narrowness comes upstream: the publisher can pass on only what the illustrator granted.
   - Corrected: replace "(L. 131-3)" at those places with "ordinary contract interpretation; upstream grant limited by L. 131-3 / L. 122-7".

4. **Art & Allposters, C-419/13, 22 Jan 2015: print is a new reproduction on a new medium.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees [MEM-H]. The case is about exhaustion of the distribution right. Printing a file on card is a reproduction anyway, so the authority is illustrative, not needed.

5. **Dior v Evora (C-337/95, 4 Nov 1997) rests on exhaustion; Tom Kabinet (C-263/18, 19 Dec 2019) means none for eBooks.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees on both [MEM-H]. The step "contract is the only basis" is correctly labelled inference. Omitted support: Dimensione Direct Sales v Knoll (C-516/13, 13 May 2015), where advertising copies falls within the distribution right [MEM-H].

6. **Closed list of exceptions; "France transposed only the judicial-sale catalogue case".** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees on the closed list [MEM-H], paragraph numbers unchecked. PARTLY on wording: L. 122-5 3° d) pre-dates the directive (1997, narrowed in 2000) and is not a transposition [MEM-M]. Corrected: "France did not use the art. 5(3)(j) option." The document cites only the AG opinion in C-590/23 (Pelham II); whether the judgment has been delivered is unknown to me.

7. **Short quotation never covers a full image** (Ass. plén. 5 Nov 1993; Cass. 4 July 1995; 7 Nov 2006). UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees [MEM-H]. "Fabris v Loudmer (1991)" is Cass. 1re civ. 22 Jan 1991; Utrillo/France 2 is Cass. 1re civ. 13 Nov 2003 [MEM-M].

8. **Renckhoff, C-161/17, 7 Aug 2018.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees [MEM-H]. The document recommends hotlinked thumbnails (row 13, rule 19) without the authorities that make hotlinking defensible: Svensson, BestWater, and VG Bild-Kunst (C-392/19, 9 Mar 2021) [MEM-H].

9. **VCAST (C-265/16), ACI Adam, Copydan and the "tool versus supplier" design rule.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees on all three [MEM-H]. Incomplete: see omission B1 (Ocilion).

10. **L. 122-5 2° text, the "fins identiques" carve-out, gift to a third party.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees on the text and on the Aurélien D. sequence [MEM-H/M]. The "fins identiques" worry is the author's speculation with no authority.

11. **"The user is the copier" when composition is client-side** (finding 5, §4.7 row 1). PARTLY [MEM-H on the 1984 wording].
   - Rannou-Graphie's criterion is holding the equipment and exploiting it by making it available to customers, not "operating it for customers". The shop was the copiste even though customers pressed the button.
   - Consequence 1: a user who prints the "print-at-home" PDF at a copy shop, kiosk or office printer is not the copiste in France. S-home/T3 holds only for the user's own printer; the document does not say so.
   - Consequence 2: "the user" should read "arguably the user".
   - Wizzgo: the decisions that matter are TGI Paris 25 Nov 2008 (merits) and CA Paris 14 Dec 2011 [MEM-M]. The document cites only a référé of 6 Nov 2008.
   - Both authorities come from one weak secondary source each (a student thesis page; one Village de la Justice article), yet they drive the architecture claim.

12. **Cass. 1re civ., 2 July 2014, n° 13-24.359 (Magnard-Vuibert).** UNSUPPORTED. I have no independent recall; date, number and publication status need Légifrance.

13. **CA Versailles, 22 Mar 2022, Zulma v Elior: parasitisme without copyright.** UNSUPPORTED. Both citations are the same law-firm blog; the damages figure was never seen.

14. **Moral rights: cropping, overprinting, recolouring, spine extension are "recognised infringements [V2]"; the publisher cannot clear them.** PARTLY [REPO].
   - The two sources cover cropping, mirroring, colourisation and cut-outs of photographs. Overprinting and spine extension are extrapolation.
   - TGI Paris 5 Oct 2007 is tagged [V1] but was seen only as a search snippet (§10.2-5).
   - Inalienability: memory agrees [MEM-H]. But an author can consent to specific, identified modifications, so finding 6 should read "cannot be cleared by the publisher alone".

15. **Sanctions and damages** (§4.2, §7.1).
   - L. 335-2, 3 years and EUR 300,000: UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees [MEM-H].
   - L. 331-1-3 "lump sum at least equal to the fee": OUTDATED [MEM-H]. Since loi 2014-315 of 11 Mar 2014 the lump sum must be higher than the royalties that would have been due, and it does not exclude moral prejudice.
   - TJ Bordeaux 26 June 2025 "three times the fee" and the "pop" EUR 15,000: UNSUPPORTED, one secondary source each.

16. **DSA arts. 6, 16, 17, 19; LCEN aligned by the 2024 SREN law.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees [MEM-H]. PARTLY on art. 6(3): it lifts the exemption only for liability under consumer-protection law, for platforms that let consumers conclude distance contracts with traders. It is not an IP rule; the document's "art. 6(3) logic" is an analogy. Better authority: omission B3.

17. **ONIX: List 160 codes 01-03, List 154 "Restricted", List 21 "MDT", no print-permission field.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees on these [MEM-H/M]. PARTLY:
   - The List 154 statement is sourced to a List 158 page (citation mismatch, inherited from the note).
   - The open question on a full-cover code: I recall List 158 code 29 "Full cover" (front, spine, back, flaps) [MEM-M].
   - "No usage constraint" is too strong: ONIX carries "From date"/"Until date" roles for resources (List 155 codes 14/15) [MEM-M], and I believe a Territory element for supporting resources [MEM-L]. The `valid_until` and `territories` fields should map to these.

18. **Precedents; "every documented precedent is rights-holder opt-in; no litigation found".** UNSUPPORTED for the Enthrill figures (3,000+ locations; about 5 %, $0.25–$1.50), the 2016 Kindle cards and the Juniper quotations.
   - Walmart–Kobo, Aug 2018, about 40 titles: memory agrees [MEM-M].
   - Out of Print has been owned by Penguin Random House since 2017 [MEM-H]; the document does not mention this.
   - The "none found" statements rest on 31 searches; Litographs and French and German precedents were not searched.
   - Leads to check [MEM-L]: publisher-issued eBook download cards in France (Bragelonne, about 2012) and Germany ("E-Book-Cards", 2012).

19. **Stock licences: 500,000-copy cap; merchandise needs an extended licence.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees for the main agencies' standard licences [MEM-M]. Vendor-specific, as the document says.

20. **Germany: § 53(1) and (6), § 58, BGH Internet-Videorecorder and Möbelkatalog.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees [MEM-H]. LG Köln 14 O 291/24: UNSUPPORTED. BGH "Parfumflakon" (I ZR 256/97, 4 May 2000) is in the independent note but was dropped from the synthesis. It lets a lawful distributor advertise with product images, which cuts against the quoted "gewisses rechtliches Risiko" for physical books [MEM-H].

21. **UK and US.** UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees on s.28B (quashed July 2015), s.30(1ZA), s.30(2), Kelly, Perfect 10, Warhol, OSU v Redbubble and Pokémon v Redbubble [MEM-H]. PARTLY:
   - The 25-year term for typographical arrangement is in s.15, not s.8.
   - Finding 4's [V2] covers thumbnails only; "not a full-size printed jacket" is analysis.
   - The Redbubble account is one-sided: Atari v Redbubble and Y.Y.G.M. v Redbubble (9th Cir. 2023) went largely for Redbubble [MEM-M].

22. **Public-domain tier T4: "Green" everywhere, death + 71 years, "same policy" for UK and US** (rules 9 and 22). WRONG outside France and most of the EU [MEM-H].
   - Spain: 80 years after death for authors who died before 7 Dec 1987.
   - US: for older works the term runs from publication; works published in 1931 or later can still be protected in 2026 whatever the artist's death date.
   - Co-authored covers: the term runs from the last surviving co-author.
   - Corrected: the rule holds for FR/DE and most of the EU; add a per-country gate.

23. **Trade marks; "demos must not use retailer names".** Referential use, BMW v Deenik, Denso Wave, Cass. com. 26 June 2024: UNSUPPORTED; memory agrees [MEM-H]. All of §6 is [M].
   - The blanket ban is a risk-averse policy presented as a legal rule. Naming a retailer descriptively is the same referential use the document relies on elsewhere; the exposure lies in copying a logo, look or look-alike name.
   - The owner's brief asks for "a fake 7switch" and "a fake fnac". The document should state where the legal line sits, not impose a blanket ban.

## B. Omissions (none web-verified; authorities named for checking)

1. **Ocilion, C-426/21, 13 July 2023** [MEM-H]. The CJEU's later ruling on private copying through a commercial provider. It limits the exception for provider-run recording, and holds that merely supplying on-premises hardware and software is not a communication to the public. It bears directly on §4.7.
2. **Filmspeler (C-527/15) and Ziggo (C-610/15)** [MEM-H]. A tool designed and marketed to give access to protected works can make the supplier primarily liable. "Exposure limited to indirect theories" is too confident.
3. **Louboutin v Amazon, C-148/21 and C-184/21, 22 Dec 2022** [MEM-H]. A platform that presents third-party goods as its own may itself be the user of the sign. This is the right authority for "rendered under our brand".
4. **Design law is absent.** The EU unregistered design right gives three years' protection against copying of a product's appearance, including graphic works and get-up [MEM-H]. It is relevant to recent covers, collection looks, and typographic covers that fail the originality test.
5. **Collective management.** Where the illustrator or photographer is an ADAGP or SAIF member, merchandising-type rights may sit with the society, not the publisher [MEM-M]. A publisher opt-in may then be ineffective; the warranty in X.2 should cover it.
6. **Source of the user's cover file.** Private copying does not permit circumventing technical protection (CPI L. 331-5 ff.) [MEM-H]. Extracting a cover from a protected EPUB is not analysed, nor are the retailer's terms of use.
7. **Reprography regime (CPI L. 122-10 ff.)** [MEM-M]. Copy-shop printing falls under the CFC scheme, and copies for sale or promotion need the author's consent.
8. **Médiateur du livre** [MEM-H]. This body, competent for loi 2011-590, is not mentioned as a route to settle the prime question.
9. **French platform cases** [MEM-L, leads]. La Martinière v Google (TGI Paris, 18 Dec 2009) on unlicensed display of book content, and SAIF v Google (CA Paris, 26 Jan 2011) on thumbnails. Check whether covers were part of either holding.
10. **Facts about named counterparties were not rechecked.** Whether 7switch still trades, whether the Fnac–Kobo arrangement is unchanged in 2026, and amendments to loi 2011-590 after 2018 (for example loi 2021-1901).
11. **Consumer-facing warranty and indemnity** may be an unfair term (C. conso. L. 212-1) [MEM-M].
