# Legal notes — using book covers without a licence (standalone mode) and what a reseller may hand over for printing

Status: independent research notes, 2026-11-12. Not legal advice; written to scope a product policy and a brief for French IP counsel.

**Method and limits (read first).** 15 web searches were completed; the session's shared search budget then ran out (5 further queries were refused) and WebFetch is blocked for the primary-source hosts I tried (`legifrance.gouv.fr`, `gesetze-im-internet.de`, `fournol-avocat.fr`). Consequences: no primary text was read in full; several classic authorities are cited from memory and tagged **[M]**. Tags: **[V2]** = seen in at least two independent sources this session, **[V1]** = one source, **[M]** = memory, unverified this session. Every link below appeared in a search result of this session.

## 1. Bottom line

1. A book cover is a protected work (often several stacked works). No statutory exception in France, in EU law generally, in Germany or in the UK allows a service to reproduce a full cover on a printed object it sells. The US fair-use case law that protects cover *thumbnails* does not stretch to a full-size printed jacket.
2. The only scenario with a credible statutory basis is the end user making one copy for private purposes from a lawful source, on the user's own equipment. This is the legal reason (not just the performance reason) to compose the PDF client-side in WASM and never to supply the cover from our servers.
3. Publisher tolerance of cover use is real but is tolerance, revocable at will, and it has only ever covered reduced-size, informational display that sells the book.
4. Recommended default: typographic (text-only) jacket generated by us; artwork only when (a) licensed through the reseller/publisher, or (b) uploaded by the user for print-at-home, client-side only. No professional printing of unlicensed covers.

## 2. What is protected in a cover

| Layer | Typical right-holder | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Illustration / photograph / painting | Illustrator, photographer, agency, estate; publisher only if assigned | Rights are "often" assigned to the publisher, who must be able to prove the assignment ([Pierrat, Village de la justice](https://www.village-justice.com/articles/tirer-couverture,54716.html)) [V1]. Cover illustration commissions are treated as a contract for services with an assignment of the reproduction right, not a publishing contract (Cass. 2 July 2014, via [ActuaLitté](https://actualitte.com/article/46544/distribution/realiser-la-seule-couverture-d-039-un-livre-n-039-oblige-pas-au-contrat-d-039-edition)) [V1]. |
| Layout / composition (maquette) | Designer or publisher | The designer of the layout is also a right-holder ([Guichet du Savoir](https://www.guichetdusavoir.org/question/voir/139502)) [V1]. Originality is not automatic: CA Versailles, 22 March 2022 (Zulma v Elior) denied copyright in the cover of "L'Embellie" for lack of demonstrated creative choices but still found **parasitisme** for its reuse in an advertising campaign ([Fournol 1](https://www.fournol-avocat.fr/actualite/2022/8/17/la-difficile-reconnaissance-de-loriginalit-des-couvertures-de-livre), [Fournol 2](https://www.fournol-avocat.fr/actualite/2022/8/18/parasitisme-reconnu-pour-la-couverture-dun-livre)) [V1]. So a "plain" cover is not safe: unfair-competition law (art. 1240 Code civil [M]) fills the gap. |
| Moral rights (FR) | Author personally, then heirs | Perpetual and inalienable (CPI L121-1) [M]. Cropping an image to fit a cover format without express consent was treated as an integrity issue (TGI Paris, 5 Oct 2007, [Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/juri/id/JURITEXT000018862984)) [V1]. Directly relevant to us: cropping, recolouring, overlaying a QR code, or extending the artwork onto a generated spine are modifications. |
| Trade marks / logos | Publisher, collection, franchise | Publisher and series logos on the cover are marks; reproducing them on a product we sell adds trade-mark and "false endorsement" exposure [M]. |
| Title | Author / publisher | In France an original title is protected, and even afterwards against confusing reuse (CPI L112-4) [M]; using the title to designate the very book is the normal referential use. |

Covers are subject to copyright and permission is in principle due from the publisher or author; right-holders "rarely object" to a reproduction smaller than the book and aimed at informing readers, but commercial use requires asking ([Guichet du Savoir 139502](https://www.guichetdusavoir.org/question/voir/139502), [45700](https://www.guichetdusavoir.org/question/voir/45700)) [V2 with Pierrat on the principle; V1 on the tolerance].

## 3. Exceptions, jurisdiction by jurisdiction

### 3.1 EU frame

- **Closed list.** Art. 5 InfoSoc Directive 2001/29 is exhaustive; Member States and courts cannot add exceptions, not even on fundamental-rights grounds: Funke Medien (C-469/17, para 64), Pelham (C-476/17, para 65), Spiegel Online (C-516/17, para 49), all 29 July 2019 ([Kluwer on Spiegel Online](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/reflections-on-the-cjeus-judgment-in-spiegel-online-is-there-a-golden-intersection-between-freedom-of-expression-and-eu-copyright-law-part-i/), [Kluwer on Pelham](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/sample-sample-in-my-song-can-they-tell-where-you-are-from-the-pelham-judgment-part-ii/), restated in the AG opinion in C-590/23, [FRA](https://fra.europa.eu/en/node/49752)) [V2]. Practical meaning: there is no "it helps sell the book" defence and no EU fair use.
- **Re-posting is a new act.** Renckhoff (C-161/17, 7 Aug 2018): copying a photo that is freely and lawfully available on one site and posting it on another is a new communication to a new public; holding otherwise would amount to exhaustion of the communication right, excluded by art. 3(3); absence of technical restrictions on the source is irrelevant ([IRIS Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/8373), [Columbia GFoE](https://globalfreedomofexpression.columbia.edu/cases/land-nordrhein-westfalen-v-dirk-renckhoff/), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/copyright/730320/the-risks-of-reposting-online-content-cjeu-rules-on-the-unauthorised-reposting-of-copyright-protected-works-on-the-internet)) [V2]. "The cover is all over the web" is therefore not a defence for a service that fetches and re-serves it.
- **Photographs are fully protected.** Painer (C-145/10): portrait photos are protected if they are the author's own intellectual creation, with no reduced protection ([IPKat](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2011/12/all-photos-are-created-equal-painer.html), [Kluwer](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/another-piece-of-the-puzzle-or-is-it-cjeu-on-photographs-as-copyright-works/)) [V2]. Painer's quotation holdings (source and author must be indicated unless impossible) are [M].
- **Private copying through a service.** VCAST (C-265/16, 29 Nov 2017): a cloud service can in principle sit inside the private-copy exception, but the user must have lawful access to the source, and where the provider itself makes the work available without authorisation (an art. 3 act) the exception fails ([Kluwer](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/up-in-the-cloud-some-reflections-on-the-cjeu-judgment-in-vcast/), [IPKat](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2017/11/the-vcast-decision-how-to-turn-private.html), [LK Shields](https://lkshields.ie/news-insights/publication/eu-court-defines-boundaries-for-cloud-based-services-recorded-tv-programmes), [Quintais/Rendas](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/26578344/EU_copyright_law_and_the_Cloud.pdf)) [V2]. Design rule derived from it: **a tool that helps the user copy something the user already has may be covered; a service that also supplies the work is not.** Private-copy regimes cannot cover unlawful sources ([Next](https://next.ink/news/86942-la-justice-europeenne-impose-decouplage-copie-privee-et-source-illicite.htm) [V1]; ACI Adam C-435/12 [M]).
- **Exhaustion** only frees further distribution of the tangible copy sold; it never authorises a new reproduction (Art & Allposters C-419/13, poster-to-canvas transfer [M]) and does not exist for eBooks at all (Tom Kabinet C-263/18 [M]). The reseller's exhaustion-based freedom to *advertise* goods it resells (Dior v Evora C-337/95; BGH "Parfumflakon" [M]) concerns advertising physical stock, not manufacturing a new object.
- Art. 5(3)(j) (advertising the exhibition or sale of artistic works, "excluding any other commercial use"), 5(3)(i) (incidental inclusion), 5(3)(k) (parody) [M] are discussed per country below. A faithful cover is not a parody (no humour, no distance: Deckmyn C-201/13 [M]).
- Art. 17 DSM Directive (content-sharing platforms) does not fit: we do not give the public access to user uploads [M].

### 3.2 France (CPI)

- **L122-4**: any reproduction without consent is unlawful; contrefaçon is also a criminal offence (L335-2: up to 3 years and EUR 300,000 [M]).
- **Short quotation (L122-5 3° a) does not work for images.** "La représentation intégrale d'une œuvre, quelles que soient sa forme et sa durée, ne peut s'analyser comme une courte citation" (Cass. 1re civ., 4 July 1995, Antenne 2, [text](https://affairesjuridiques.aphp.fr/textes/cour-de-cassation-civile-1ere-4-juillet-1995-antenne-2-droits-dauteur-courte-citation-representation-integrale-de-loeuvre/)); a photograph reproduced in reduced format is still a full reproduction (Cass. 1re civ., 7 Nov 2006, [Squire Patton Boggs](https://larevue.squirepattonboggs.com/droits-d-auteur-reproduction-d-une-photographie_a334.html)); same line in Fabris v Loudmer (1991) and the Utrillo/France 2 case ([IRIS Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/284)); summarised by [Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/citations-dartistes) [V2]. A thumbnail-sized cover is not a "citation" in French law.
- **Accessory theory / inclusion fortuite.** Cass. 1re civ., 12 May 2011, n° 08-20.651 ("Être et avoir"): illustrations visible in the background of a documentary were an accessory inclusion outside the monopoly ([Squire Patton Boggs](https://larevue.squirepattonboggs.com/etre-et-avoir-ou-les-auxiliaires-du-droit-d-auteur_a1468.html), [Le Monde du Droit](https://www.lemondedudroit.fr/pi-tic/278-propriete-intellectuelle-industrielle/33575-affaire-qetre-et-avoirq-de-linclusion-fortuite-dune-oeuvre.html), [IRIS Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/5813)) [V2]. On our card the cover is the main subject of the front panel, so the theory is unavailable (my application).
- **Private copy (L122-5 2°).** Text: copies "réalisées à partir d'une source licite et strictement réservées à l'usage privé du copiste et non destinées à une utilisation collective" ([Baumann](https://www.baumann-avocats.com/CPI/article-l122-5-du-code-de-la-propriete-intellectuelle.php), [Doctrine](https://www.doctrine.fr/l/texts/codes/LEGITEXT000006069414/sections/LEGISCTA000006179045)) [V2]. Three difficulties:
  1. *Who is the copiste?* Under the Rannou-Graphie line (CA Paris 8 Oct 1982; Cass. 1re civ. 7 March 1984) the copier is the party operating the equipment for customers, not the customer ([thesis summary](https://www.memoireonline.com/12/06/297/m_l-exception-de-copie-privee-face-aux-dispositifs-techniques-de-protection-des-oeuvres10.html)) [V1]; the online recorder Wizzgo could not shelter behind its users' private copying (TGI Paris, réf., 6 Nov 2008, [Village de la justice](https://www.village-justice.com/articles/affaire-wizzgo-prend-tournant,4999.html)) [V1]. A PDF rendered on our server therefore makes *us* the copier; a PDF rendered in the user's browser on the user's device keeps the user as copier.
  2. *"Usage privé du copiste"*: a card handed to a friend is not obviously the copier's own use. Unsettled; I found no case [M].
  3. The 2° carve-out for "copies des œuvres d'art destinées à être utilisées pour des fins identiques à celles pour lesquelles l'œuvre originale a été créée" [M]: a cover reproduced to serve as a cover on a shelf is uncomfortably close.
- No general French equivalent of art. 5(3)(j); the only catalogue exception is for judicial-sale catalogues (L122-5 3° d) [M].
- **Licence scope is read narrowly**: an assignment covers only the modes of exploitation it lists (L131-3) [M]. A reseller's right to display the cover on its product page does not, by itself, include printing it on a physical gift object.
- Litigation climate: French courts do sanction image reuse by commercial sites, e.g. damages set at three times the evaded licence fee for a photo reused on a third-party site (TJ Bordeaux, 26 June 2025, per [Kohen Avocats](https://kohenavocats.com/concurrent-copie-site-internet-textes-photos-fiches-faire-cesser-chiffrer-2026/)) [V1]; systematic reuse of auction-catalogue content led to a contrefaçon and parasitisme judgment against Artprice ([UGGC](https://www.uggc.com/condamnation-dartprice-com-pour-contrefacon-et-concurrence-parasitaire/)) [V1]; Moulinsart obtained an infringement finding over reproduction of Tintin cover elements (Cass. 1re civ., 26 May 2011, n° 09-71.083, [Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/juri/id/JURITEXT000024085051/), facts taken from a search summary only) [V1].

### 3.3 Germany (UrhG, [statute](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/BJNR012730965.html))

- **§ 51 (Zitat)**: a Bildzitat needs an inner link between image and text; a cover used as decoration next to a review does not qualify, and bloggers "in der Regel" hold no rights in the cover ([WBS 1](https://www.wbs.legal/it-und-internet-recht/verwendung-der-fotografie-eines-buchcovers-im-rahmen-einer-buchrezension-ohne-zustimmung-des-urhebers-14143/), [WBS 2](https://www.wbs.legal/internetrecht/fotorecht-bildrecht/verwendung-der-fotografie-eines-buchcovers-im-rahmen-einer-buchrezension-ohne-zustimmung-des-urhebers-32292/)) [V1]. Author credit is a substantive condition of the quotation defence (LG Köln, 14 O 291/24, per [Ferner](https://www.ferner-alsdorf.de/lg-koeln-notwendigkeit-der-urheberangabe-bei-der-berufung-auf-die-zitatschranke/)) [V1].
- **§ 58 (Katalogbildfreiheit)**: lets the *organiser* (gallery, art dealer, auction house) reproduce and put online works that are publicly exhibited or intended for public sale, as far as needed to promote the event ([Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/id/beitrag/2-urheberrecht-c-werke-in-ausstellungen-HI15189406.html), [§ 58 text](https://lexmea.de/de/gesetz/urhg/58)) [V1]. It is about selling the artwork itself. An analogous application to covers is rejected as soon as the site has a commercial purpose ([WBS](https://www.wbs.legal/internetrecht/fotorecht-bildrecht/verwendung-der-fotografie-eines-buchcovers-im-rahmen-einer-buchrezension-ohne-zustimmung-des-urhebers-32292/)) [V1]; traders who advertise with book-cover images "tragen ein gewisses rechtliches Risiko" and rely on lived practice ([IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/Urheberrecht-Versto%C3%9F-Produktfotos-Produktbeschreibung-Werke.html)) [V1]. § 58 gives nothing for a printed product.
- **§ 53(1)**: single copies by a natural person for private use, not from an obviously unlawful source; may be made by a third party if free of charge or if on paper by photomechanical or similar processes; copies may not be distributed to the public (§ 53(6)); private use includes the close personal circle; for automated services the customer who triggers the process is the maker (BGH "Internet-Videorecorder") — all [M]. Germany is the most favourable jurisdiction for scenario 1.
- **§ 57 (unwesentliches Beiwerk)**: only for elements that could be swapped without anyone noticing (BGH "Möbelkatalog") [M]; a cover on the front panel is the opposite.
- Enforcement: image Abmahnungen (cease-and-desist with contractual penalty, damages, lawyer's fees) are routine ([IT-Recht Kanzlei FAQ](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/abmahnung-urheberrecht-bilderklau-faq.html)) [V1]. Highest practical enforcement risk of the five jurisdictions.

### 3.4 United Kingdom (CDPA 1988)

- Fair dealing is purpose-bound. Quotation (s.30(1ZA), added 2014): work already public, fair dealing, no more than required, sufficient acknowledgement ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/30), [2014 Regulations](https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2356/regulation/3/made?view=plain), [UCL](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/copyright/2023/02/24/fair-dealing-week-2023-part-2-three-fair-dealing-exceptions)) [V2]. It formally reaches photographs, but it is hard to see a whole image qualifying; news reporting excludes photographs (s.30(2)) [V1].
- No private-copying exception: s.28B was quashed in 2015 [M]. s.63 (advertising the sale of an artistic work) and s.31 (incidental inclusion) do not fit [M]. Published editions also carry a 25-year typographical-arrangement right (s.8) [M].

### 3.5 United States (comparison)

- **Thumbnails are fair use for search/identification**: Kelly v Arriba Soft, 336 F.3d 811 (9th Cir. 2003); Perfect 10 v Amazon (9th Cir. 2007) ([opinion](https://m.openjurist.org/336/f3d/811), [CRS RL33810](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL33810.html)) [V2]; library associations (ALA, ARL) supported that position as amici ([EFF brief](https://eff.org/files/filenode/Perfect10_v_Google/effperfect10vgoogleamicus.pdf)) [V1]. This is the basis on which catalogues show small covers; LibraryThing states that showing covers for sale, rental or commentary is fair use "in most circumstances" ([LibraryThing blog](https://blog.librarything.com/category/covers/)) [V1] (a site's opinion, not a ruling).
- The reasoning rests on small, low-resolution images serving a different function. A full-size, print-resolution cover on a decorative object sold for money has the same expressive purpose as the original; after Warhol v Goldsmith (2023) [M] that weighs against fair use.
- **First sale** (17 USC § 109) limits only the distribution right in a particular lawful copy and is never a defence to reproduction ([DOJ manual](https://www.justice.gov/usam/title9/crm01854.htm), [Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/?p=1446)) [V2].
- Print-on-demand intermediaries are not reliably "passive": the Sixth Circuit reversed a finding that Redbubble was a mere transactional intermediary (OSU v Redbubble, 2021, trade mark) ([Fross Zelnick](https://www.frosszelnick.com/u-s-court-of-appeals-for-the-sixth-circuit-redbubble-could-be-liable-for-direct-infringement-arising-from-print-on-demand-service/), [Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2021/02/ip-lawsuits-against-print-on-demand-vendors-continue-to-vex-the-courts-osu-v-redbubble-more.htm)) [V2]; in Australia Redbubble was held to infringe copyright, with nominal damages (Pokémon v Redbubble [2017] FCA 1541, [WILMap](https://wilmap.stanford.edu/node/30497)) [V1]. I found no EU judgment on print-on-demand platforms.

## 4. Scenarios

| # | Scenario | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | User prints one card at home for a gift | Best case: user supplies the cover file (from the eBook they bought, or their own scan), composition runs in the browser, nothing is uploaded. Private-copy arguments are then the *user's* (strong in DE, arguable in FR, unavailable in UK, plausible fair use in US). Our role is that of a tool supplier. If we render server-side we become the copier in French doctrine (Rannou-Graphie, Wizzgo); if we fetch the cover for the user we make it available ourselves (VCAST, Renckhoff) and the user's exception no longer protects us. |
| 2 | Service prints and ships commercially | Reproduction and distribution by a business, for payment. No exception anywhere in the EU or UK; weak fair use in the US. Requires a licence or right-holder status of the customer (author, publisher). |
| 3 | Preview only | User-supplied file previewed in the user's own browser: no communication to the public by us. Service-fetched cover shown as a thumbnail: unlicensed communication in EU terms (Renckhoff), tolerated in practice, fair use in the US. Use only feeds whose terms allow it (e.g. Amazon data requires a link-back per [LibraryThing](https://blog.librarything.com/category/covers/); Goodreads' own librarians were told covers from Amazon or other booksellers could not legally be reused, [Goodreads thread](https://www.goodreads.com/topic/show/1277733-source-of-book-covers?page=1)) [V1]. |
| 4 | Public-domain text, recent edition artwork | The text's status says nothing about the cover. New illustration or photo: protected for the artist's life + 70 years; French moral rights survive that. A purely typographic modern cover may lack originality in France (Zulma/Elior) but parasitisme remains. Safe variant: first-edition or period cover whose artist died more than 70 years ago, from a source without contractual restrictions. |
| 5 | User-designed or text-only cover | Title, author name, publisher name as text, our own layout and licensed fonts: no third-party artwork. Residual points: no publisher logos, no imitation of a recognisable series design (parasitisme), user warranty for uploaded own artwork. Lowest risk; should be the default. |

## 6. Intermediary liability and notice handling

- **DSA (Reg. 2022/2065).** Art. 6 keeps the hosting exemption for providers that store information at the user's request, have no actual knowledge and act expeditiously once aware; it is lost where the provider plays an active role giving it knowledge or control (line from Google France and L'Oréal v eBay); injunctions remain possible (art. 6(4)); art. 6(3) removes it for marketplaces that make consumers believe the product comes from the platform itself ([Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/the-digital-services-act-new-liability-rules-102iiyf), [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/fr/interface/2022/the-eus-digital-services-act/online-intermediaries-and-illegal-content-under-the-digital-services-act), [BEUC](https://www.beuc.eu/sites/default/files/publications/ConsumerPro_DSA-complement_to_TBD_2023.pdf), [Busch report](https://digitalplanet.tufts.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/DD-Report_2-Christoph-Busch-11.30.22.pdf)) [V2].
- **Art. 16 notice-and-action**: every hosting provider needs an electronic notice mechanism; a sufficiently precise notice gives actual knowledge; decisions must be timely, diligent, non-arbitrary and objective ([Burges Salmon](https://burges-salmon.com/news-and-insight/legal-updates/data-protection/the-eu-digital-services-act-and-the-article-6-hosting-exemption), [art. 16 text](https://www.springlex.eu/fr/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-16/)) [V2]. Contact points (arts. 11–12), statement of reasons (art. 17), small-enterprise carve-out from platform-only duties (art. 19) [M].
- **Where hosting status helps us and where it does not.** It can cover a user-uploaded cover stored at the user's request. It cannot cover (i) covers we select or fetch by ISBN (our own content), (ii) printing and shipping, where we manufacture the object (Redbubble analogy), (iii) anything rendered under our brand as our product (art. 6(3) logic). Client-side composition is stronger than a safe harbour: nothing is hosted.
- **France (LCEN, loi 2004-575, art. 6)**: hosting regime and notice formalities now aligned on the DSA since the 2024 "SREN" law [M].
- **US**: DMCA § 512 safe harbour for user-stored content requires a registered agent and a repeat-infringer policy [M]; many print-on-demand sites follow it ([IPWatchdog](https://www.ipwatchdog.com/2021/03/09/litigation-threatening-burst-redbubble-courts-weigh-ip-implications-redbubbles-unique-online-marketplace/id=130712/)) [V1].

## 8. Mitigations

| Mitigation | Effect | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Client-side (WASM) composition, no server copy of covers | Keeps the user as the only copier; removes hosting question | Already the architecture |
| Typographic jacket as default and universal fallback | Removes third-party artwork entirely | Design work (templates, fonts) |
| Licence through reseller contract: explicit clause for "printed gift jacket / carte cadeau imprimée", warranty and indemnity from the reseller, per-title flag in the feed | Clears scenario 2 for participating titles | Negotiation; depends on publishers' upstream grants (L131-3) |
| Publisher opt-in programme (allow-list by ISBN prefix; free, non-exclusive, revocable, purpose-limited licence; no alteration; credit line) | Turns tolerance into a licence | Outreach; start with independent publishers and self-published authors |
| Opt-out / blocklist by ISBN prefix, honoured quickly | Limits exposure with litigious right-holders | Low |
| Notice-and-action process (DSA art. 16 form, logged decisions, contact point, DMCA agent if US traffic) | Preserves safe harbour where it applies; shows good faith | Low |
| No alteration of artwork: no crop, recolour or overlay; spine and back are separate panels in our own typography; QR only inside; credit "Couverture : © …" | Reduces moral-rights and parasitisme claims | Layout constraint |
| User terms: warranty of rights or private use, one print per gift code, no resale | Supports the private-copy narrative; weak as indemnity against consumers | Low |
| No scraping of retailer or aggregator sites for print assets; thumbnails only from APIs whose terms allow display | Avoids Renckhoff, database-right and contract claims | Fewer covers in preview |
| Written opinion from French IP counsel before enabling pro-print with third-party covers | Tests the points marked uncertain | One-off fee |

## 9. Recommended default product policy

1. **Default output: typographic jacket** (title, author, publisher name as text; our fonts; no logos), for every mode and every print route.
2. **Artwork tier A — licensed**: cover is used on printed cards (home or professional) only for titles flagged as cleared by a reseller contract or the publisher opt-in list.
3. **Artwork tier B — user-supplied, print-at-home only**: the user uploads a cover into the browser; composition is client-side; the file never reaches our servers; the user confirms private, non-commercial use; one card per gift code. Not offered for professional printing.
4. **Professional printing with artwork** only for tier A, or when the orderer is the right-holder (author, publisher) and warrants it — the Juniper Custom rule.
5. **We never fetch a cover into a printable artefact** in standalone mode. ISBN lookup returns text metadata; a thumbnail may be shown in the UI only from a source whose terms allow it, and is not embedded in the PDF.
6. **Covers are never modified.** The 10 mm spine is generated from text; it does not extend, sample or imitate the cover artwork unless the title is in tier A and the licence allows adaptation.
7. **Public-domain titles**: treat the cover as a separate work; offer period or first-edition covers only when the artist's death date clears life + 70.
8. **Takedown**: public notice form, same-day removal from allow-lists and caches, ISBN-prefix blocklist, log of notices and decisions.
9. **UK and US users**: same policy; do not rely on fair use or fair dealing for anything printed.
