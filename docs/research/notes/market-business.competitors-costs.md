Reasons given in the trade press for the failures:

- **Nobody wanted to buy, "much less redeem", the card** — the wording used about the Target pilot ([The Digital Reader](https://the-digital-reader.com/ebook-startup-livrada-shut/)) [V1]. A 2016 trade blog concluded that "individualized ebook gift cards" had not taken off ([The eBook Reader Blog](https://blog.the-ebook-reader.com/2016/01/22/kindle-cards-new-book-specific-kindle-gift-cards)) [V1].
- **Price mismatch**: the Kindle Card for *The Martian* cost USD 14.99 on the rack while the Kindle edition sold for USD 8.99 (one outlet in the January 2016 coverage — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2016/amazons-other-physical-retail-test-testing-the-kindle-e-book-kiosk-at-the-local-drug-store/) / [Consumerist](https://consumerist.com/2016/01/25/amazon-puts-mini-e-book-stores-in-drugstores-in-washington-state); excerpt not pinned to one page) [V1]. A fixed printed price cannot follow a dynamic eBook price.
- **Tiny assortment** (20–40 titles) versus an online catalogue of millions; rack space and inventory are the scarce resource in that model [V2, same sources as table].
- **Dependence on one retailer**: Livrada reportedly ran out of money after Target stopped ([BookWorks](https://www.bookworks.com/2018/08/walmart-ebook-gift-card-launch-doomed/); second-hand) [V1].
- **Staff ignorance / no merchandising** at Walmart (forum reports) [V1].

### 1.4 Lessons for eBook Cadeau

1. **Inventory-based retail racks failed four times** (Livrada, Enthrill, Kindle Cards, Walmart/Kobo). The surviving forms are on-demand: e-mail gifting at checkout, and author-printed codes. On-demand generation of a card for any title is the structural difference to preserve; pre-printed stock for retail racks should not be on the roadmap.
2. **Never print a price or a stored value** on the object: the card must represent a specific prepaid order, not money (avoids the USD 14.99 vs 8.99 problem and keeps it away from stored-value products).
3. **Redemption friction killed the earlier products.** The earlier cards required account creation plus code typing on a different device. The QR-plus-short-code path must land on the retailer's "add to my library" step in one or two actions.
4. **The paying customer who persists is the author/publisher** (BookFunnel, Dropcards, Bookwire). Packs for signings and fairs are the segment with a proven willingness to pay; consumer willingness to pay for a title-specific card is unproven.
5. **The gift object itself was weak**: a plastic card on a peg is not a present. A jacket with cover and spine that stands on a shelf is an untested differentiator, not a validated one.
6. **Single-partner dependence is fatal** (Livrada/Target, Enthrill/Walmart): keep the standalone mode viable on its own.

**Derived design constraint (my arithmetic, not sourced).** A pocket-format jacket (110 + 10 + 110 mm wide, 178 mm high, about 0.041 m²) weighs about 10 g at 250 g/m², 12 g at 300 g/m² and 14 g at 350 g/m². A C5 envelope adds roughly 7–8 g. So: 250 g/m² stays under 20 g; 300 g/m² is borderline; 350 g/m², any flaps, or a large-format jacket (135 × 215 mm covers, about 18 g at 300 g/m²) falls into the 100 g band. The band jump costs **EUR 1.58 per card in 2026 and EUR 1.67 in 2027**, i.e. as much as the whole stamp. Paper weight and format are therefore a pricing decision, not only a print decision. Weigh real samples before fixing the range.

**Derived micro-ticket effect.** The EUR 0.25 fixed part dominates on small baskets: with Stripe, a EUR 1.99 sale costs EUR 0.28 (14%), EUR 4.90 costs EUR 0.32 (6.6%), EUR 9.90 costs EUR 0.40 (4.0%). A percentage-only tariff (2.5%) is cheaper than 1.5% + EUR 0.25 for any basket under EUR 25. A paid "premium PDF" at EUR 1–2 is therefore structurally unattractive when sold one at a time; prepaid credit packs (the Fizzer pattern) or monthly B2B invoicing avoid the fixed fee.

Structural point (derived): micro charges apply to **turnover, including re-billed postage and print**. On a printed-and-mailed card where roughly 55–60% of the price is pass-through cost, 12.3% of turnover is about 30% of the value added. Micro is fine for validation and for the SaaS/API fee line; a mail-order card line at volume belongs in a company that deducts costs. Which of the three rates applies to each revenue line (printed card = goods; API fee = BIC service) is my reading [M] and should be confirmed with URSSAF or an accountant.

**Illustrative unit economics, printed and mailed card, France, 2027 stamp, pocket format ≤ 20 g, price EUR 4.90** (derived; print cost EUR 1.00 and envelope EUR 0.08 are placeholders):

| Line | Micro-entreprise, no VAT | Company, 20% VAT |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue kept | 4.90 | 4.08 excl. VAT |
| Stamp (Lettre verte 20 g, 2027) | −1.67 | −1.67 |
| Print + envelope | −1.08 | −1.08 |
| Payment (1.5% + 0.25) | −0.32 | −0.32 |
| Social charges 12.3% of turnover | −0.60 | — |
| **Contribution per card** | **about 1.23** | **about 1.01** (before overheads and corporate tax) |

If the card exceeds 20 g (stamp EUR 3.34), the same EUR 4.90 price gives a negative contribution; about EUR 6.90 is needed to get back to about EUR 1.3. With lean yearly cash costs of roughly EUR 600–1,300 (hosting about EUR 130–300 [V1], plus domain, insurance, bank, CFE [M]) and excluding the owner's time, break-even is in the order of **500–1,050 mailed cards a year**, or **2,000–4,300 cards a year at a EUR 0.30 B2B fee** [derived from partly unverified inputs].

**Regulatory note affecting pricing [M]:** French law (loi du 26 mai 2011) lets the publisher fix the eBook retail price, so a reseller cannot fund the card by discounting the eBook; the card has to be a separately priced service or a free option absorbed by the reseller. Detailed treatment belongs to the legal notes.

## 5. Synthesis

- The concept has a clear record of failure in its inventory-based retail form and a clear record of survival in two on-demand forms. eBook Cadeau sits in the surviving family, provided it stays zero-inventory and any-title.
- The mailed consumer product is squeezed between a EUR 1.67 stamp (2027) and a EUR 3–4.3 market price for cards; it only works for a light pocket-format jacket or at a premium price above mass-market cards.
- The cheapest route to revenue is B2B (authors/publishers first, resellers second), invoiced, with the free print-at-home tool as the demo and acquisition channel.
- Biggest unknowns: checkout attach rate; whether Fnac/Kobo or 7switch can issue a giftable entitlement at all; per-piece print quotes; affiliate rates.
