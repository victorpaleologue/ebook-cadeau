# Market size and demand — research notes

Topic: "Market, competitors, cost inputs and business-model options" — angle: MARKET SIZE AND DEMAND.
Date of research: 2026-11-12. Figures are the latest found (data years 2024 and 2025).

Confidence markers: **[V2]** verified in two or more sources, **[V1]** single source (or several outlets relaying one origin), **[M]** from memory, not verified in this session. Anything labelled ASSUMPTION is mine and is not a finding.

## 0. Method and limits (read first)

- 16 web searches were completed (FR, DE, NL, ES, IT, EN). Four more were refused because the workflow-wide search budget ran out. WebFetch was not used, so every figure comes from search-result extracts, not from reading the primary PDFs in full.
- Consequence: three parts of the brief are not covered by evidence and are filled with labelled assumptions only: (a) the share of book purchases made as gifts (FR, DE, UK), (b) self-publishing volumes (KDP, Librinova, BoD), (c) recent e-reader unit sales and retailer market shares (Amazon vs Kobo/Fnac vs Tolino vs Vivlio).
- No survey was found that asks why people do not gift eBooks. The "you cannot wrap an eBook" problem is supported only by press commentary and a US print-preference survey.
- National statistics are not comparable with each other: some are publisher net revenue, some are consumer sell-out, some include professional/academic digital. See the definitions column below.

New print books: 307 M copies (-2.5%), EUR 3.9 bn (-1.5%) [V1 — Livres Hebdo, relayed by CCFI and a library network] ([Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/marche-du-livre-baisse-des-ventes-et-polarisation-accrue-en-2025), [CCFI](https://www.ccfi.asso.fr/marche-du-livre-baisse-des-ventes-et-polarisation-accrue-en-2025/)).

### 2.3 Readers, buyers, devices (14th Baromètre Sofia/SNE/SGDL, Médiamétrie, usage year 2025)

- 47 M people (8 in 10 aged 6+) read or listened to a book in 2025; **14 M (1 in 4) read at least one digital book**; 10 M listened to a digital audiobook [V2] ([SNE](https://www.sne.fr/actu/resultats-du-barometre-2026-des-usages-dachat-et-de-lecture-des-livres-imprimes-numeriques-et-audio/), [Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/barometre-sofiasnesgdl-2026-des-usages-en-recomposition-pour-les-livres-imprimes-numeriques)). A secondary summary gives 14.4 M and 10.1 M [V1] ([JOMO Horizon](https://horizon-jomo.eklablog.com/2026/04/barometre-sofia-sne-sgdl-des-usages-d-achat-et-de-lecture-des-livres-imprimes-numeriques-et-audio-2026.html)).
- 20% say they bought at least one digital book, against 71% for print [V1 — seen in a slide extract of the [barometer PDF](https://www.sgdl.org/images/phocadownload/Baromètres/BarometreSOFIA-SNE-SGDL_2026.VDEF.pdf) and in the blog above; not read in full].
- Devices used by digital readers: **smartphone 48%, liseuse (e-reader) 34%, tablet 33%** [V1] ([SGDL](https://www.sgdl.org/sgdl-accueil/le-guide-pratique/ressources-documentaires/barometres-et-observatoire/4867-barometre-sgdl-sofia-sne-2026-des-usages-dachat-et-de-lecture-des-livres-imprimes-numeriques-et-audio)). Derived: ~4.9 M people in France read on an e-reader (34% of 14.4 M). Two thirds of digital readers therefore do not use an e-reader: the redemption flow must work on a phone first.
- Where digital books are bought: websites of large specialist chains ("grandes surfaces spécialisées", i.e. the Fnac type) 33%, digital platforms 29%. Pay-per-title is still the most cited way to obtain a digital book (21%), with subscriptions rising [V1] (same SGDL page).
- Piracy: 6% of the French (~3 M), 17% among digital readers [V1] ([SNE](https://www.sne.fr/actu/resultats-du-barometre-2026-des-usages-dachat-et-de-lecture-des-livres-imprimes-numeriques-et-audio/)).
- Caution on buyer counts: 20% declared buyers would be 11-12 M people, i.e. only ~1.5 paid eBooks each against NielsenIQ's 17 M units, whereas the German purchase panel finds 10.9 eBooks per buyer. Declared surveys overstate. ASSUMPTION: 2-4 M regular paying eBook buyers in France.

## 5. Gifting demand

### 5.1 Books as gifts and seasonality (France)

- December was 17% of annual book revenue in 2017 and 2018, with weeks 50 and 51 alone at 11%; December reached 21% in 2020 [V2] ([NielsenIQ 2018](https://nielseniq.com/global/fr/insights/report/2019/marche-du-livre-2018-une-industrie-de-4mds-e/), [NielsenIQ 2017](https://nielseniq.com/global/fr/news-center/2018/les-francais-ont-achete-plus-de-350-millions-de-livres-en-2017/), [NielsenIQ 2021](https://nielseniq.com/global/fr/insights/report/2021/tendances-du-marche-livre-en-france-formats-physiques-et-numeriques/)). The last week of 2024 weighed unusually heavily in a declining year [V1] ([Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/comment-noel-sauve-le-marche-du-livre-2024-selon-gfk)). The December share for 2025 was not found.
- GfK describes the book as the main physical entertainment product bought at Christmas in France, Italy and Spain [V1] ([ActuaLitté](https://actualitte.com/article/103491/economie/le-livre-roi-des-achats-a-noel-sur-fond-de-croissance-du-marche-jeunesse)).
- Book buyers purchase on average 2.6 books a year to give, on top of books for themselves [V1] ([L'ObSoCo for the SLF, 2026](https://lobsoco.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/LObSoCo_SLF-2026_Rapport-danalyse-6-juin.pdf)). Only 8% of buyers of new books buy exclusively for themselves, against a quarter of second-hand buyers who never buy to give [V1, secondary] ([Chasse aux livres](https://www.chasse-aux-livres.fr/pourquoi-acheter-des-livres-d-occasion)).
- Christmas gift budget 2025: EUR 243.60 on average, -7% [V1] ([The Media Leader, Havas Market](https://fr.themedialeader.com/?p=205798)).
- The share of book purchases made as gifts was not found for any country. Working value: 15-25% of print units [M].

### 5.2 State of eBook gifting and the "cannot wrap it" problem

- Press diagnosis is old and unchanged: ActuaLitté's "L'impossible cadeau du livre numérique" notes the book is an object one gives while an eBook is easy to buy for oneself and awkward to give [V1] ([ActuaLitté](https://actualitte.com/article/69820/usages-numeriques/l-039-impossible-cadeau-du-livre-numerique)); The Conversation: an eBook is a file and a usage right, "il ne fait pas un cadeau très remarquable" [V1] ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/pourquoi-les-livres-papier-nont-ils-pas-disparu-118617)).
- What exists today: Amazon.fr lets a buyer gift a Kindle book [V1] ([idboox](https://www.idboox.com/news-livres/amazon-on-peut-enfin-offrir-un-livre-numerique-kindle/)); Decitre has an "Offrir" button with a message and a chosen send date, delivered by e-mail [V1] ([Decitre FAQ](https://www.decitre.fr/faq/numerique/offrir-un-ebook)); a 2026 guide states Kobo still has no native "send as gift" function [V1, low-authority source] ([ebook-enligne.fr](https://ebook-enligne.fr/blog/offrir-ebook-guide-cadeau-numerique/)). All existing flows are e-mail only; none produces a physical object.
- Closest survey evidence (US, retailer-commissioned): 95% of book gifters would give a printed title rather than a digital one and 89% prefer to receive print (Talker Research for ThriftBooks, 2025, n=2,000) [V1] ([ThriftBooks](https://www.thriftbooks.com/blog/which-books-are-the-most-popular-holiday-gifts-this-year/)). This shows the size of the barrier; it does not show that a printed jacket removes it.

## 6. Gift cards and breakage (France)

- Market size: ~EUR 8.6 bn in 2025, projected ~EUR 10.8 bn in 2029 (Buybox); other estimates range from EUR 5 bn to EUR 8 bn depending on scope [V2 for the order of magnitude] ([Buybox](https://www.buybox.net/en/blog/the-gift-card-market), [Ecommerce Nation](https://www.ecommerce-nation.fr/futur-cartes-cadeaux-france/), [Place des Cartes via Social CSE](https://socialcse.fr/place-des-cartes-redistribue-1-mde-aux-francais/)).
- Unused share: 20-25% (Sia Partners), 20% (Place des Cartes), up to a third when gift boxes are included; EUR 1-1.8 bn lost per year [V2] ([Europe 1](https://www.europe1.fr/societe/cartes-et-cheques-cadeaux-comment-loubli-de-leur-utilisation-constitue-un-business-juteux-pour-les-enseignes-4223693), [Sleeping Money](https://www.sleepingmoney.com/fr-fr/etudes/achat-revente-cartes-cadeaux-france/), [Sud Radio](https://www.sudradio.fr/quest-ce-qui-cloche-2/cartes-et-coffrets-cadeaux-que-faire-quand-la-date-est-depassee)). Sources are industry players, not a public statistic.
- Relevance: expect 10-20% of eBook gift codes never to be redeemed (ASSUMPTION). Who keeps that money is a legal and reputational question, and a product goal (a title-specific card with a one-scan redemption should have lower breakage than a value card).

## 7. Self-publishing and author-issued cards

Nothing could be searched for this segment. No figure below is a finding.

- Self-publishing is a meaningful share of French legal deposit of printed books (order of 15-20%) [M]. KDP does not publish author counts [M].
- ASSUMPTION for sizing: 10,000-20,000 French self-published authors active at signings and "salons du livre"; 2-5% adopt; 50 cards each a year gives roughly **10,000-50,000 cards a year** for France. The use case ("dédicace" of an eBook on a printed jacket at a book fair) has no physical substitute today, so willingness to pay per card is probably higher than in the reseller channel, but volume is small.

Cross-checks:
- France base TAM of 1.0 M equals 0.21 gifted eBooks a year per e-reader user (4.9 M) or 0.07 per digital reader (14.4 M). Plausible, not aggressive.
- Against print: if 15-25% of 307 M new print copies are gifts [M], that is 46-77 M gifted print books; the base TAM is 1.3-2.2% of it.
- Value: the gifted eBooks themselves are worth about EUR 10 M a year at base TAM in France (EUR 10 per title, gifts skew to new releases; ASSUMPTION). The service revenue pool is far smaller: at EUR 0.50 (digital fee) to EUR 4 (printed card) per card, the France base SOM of 27,000 cards is **EUR 13,000-106,000 a year**; the five-country base SOM is EUR 43,000-340,000; only the five-country high case reaches EUR 0.25-2.0 M.

Seasonality overlay (ASSUMPTION built on section 5.1 and 3): 45-55% of yearly card volume falls between 20 November and 24 December, with a redemption spike on 25 December when e-readers are also unwrapped. Professional print has a cut-off around mid-December; print-at-home covers 20-24 December.

## 9. What this means for the business model

1. The market is a stable niche. Consumer eBooks are 5-6.5% of trade value everywhere and flat; average ticket EUR 7-10.5. A EUR 3-4 printed card adds 30-50% to the average eBook price, so the card must be either free (print at home), bundled by the reseller as a conversion feature, or sold on premium new releases.
2. Revenue per card is small and volume is capped, so fixed costs must stay near zero (in-browser WASM generation fits this) or revenue must come from a per-reseller licence rather than per-card fees.
3. France favours the reseller mode: specialist-chain websites are the most cited place to buy digital books (33%). Germany is the larger prize (2.2 times France's units; the Tolino alliance of physical booksellers claims 44%).
4. Design for the phone first: 48% of French digital readers read on a smartphone, 34% on an e-reader.
5. The author/signing segment is small but has no substitute and is not dependent on a reseller's roadmap; it is the cheapest place to validate willingness to pay.
